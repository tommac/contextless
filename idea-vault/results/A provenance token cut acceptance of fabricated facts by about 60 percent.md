A workshop position paper attaches how-provenance polynomials to retrieved facts. Across three open-weight models, three prompt phrasings and sixteen query sets, a bare provenance token cut acceptance of fabricated facts by roughly 60% relative to an undifferentiated fact list, at a recall cost of a few points, and beat a flat source-lineage baseline. The numbers are preliminary, and substituting trust values into the polynomial is left to future work. For this project it is early evidence that marking where each fact comes from changes what a reader accepts.
