# Database and neighbouring-field patterns for building LLM context from long conversations (notes as of 2026-11-18)

Reading conventions. Target system assumed throughout: every turn stored verbatim, paragraph-sized immutable spans with stable ids, prompts built by deterministic code from stemmed BM25, exact-phrase and value lanes, no embeddings and no model calls at query time. Tags on findings: **[MEASURED]** numbers reported by the source; **[PROPOSED]** design or argument without measurement; **[CLASSIC]** pre-LLM database or IR result; **[VENDOR]** product blog or docs; **[SNIPPET]** figure seen only in a search summary or third-party review, not in the primary text. Model dependence: **[NO MODEL]** rules, counts and lexical statistics only; **[NER]** a small local tagger such as spaCy, no LLM and no embeddings; **[EMB]** embeddings needed at query time; **[RERANK]** neural reranker at query time; **[LLM-W]** LLM at write time; **[LLM-Q]** LLM at query time. arXiv identifiers of the form 26xx are 2026 preprints unless a venue is given. Sibling notes in this folder already cover budgeted selection, compression, benchmarks and memory data structures; this note stays on the database and IR angle and repeats a sibling fact only when a new detail matters.

## Query processing: decomposition and joins over text spans, multi-hop retrieval without a model (RM3, Rocchio, iterative lexical expansion, entity or term co-occurrence bridges), measured results and when feedback hurts

### Takeaway
Classic pseudo-relevance feedback is measured to hurt bridge-style multi-hop retrieval at small cutoffs (HotpotQA R@10 0.742 to 0.675 with RM3) and adds at most 1.6 points at depth 500, while on a long conversational haystack one light feedback pass helped (LongMemEval-S +6.0 accuracy points) and on LoCoMo it was neutral. Entity or rare-key second-hop expansion is the only model-free multi-hop device with repeated evidence, its measured gains are small, and no measured model-free query decomposition or span-join method for conversational memory was found.

### Cited Findings

**Feedback on multi-hop document benchmarks (when it hurts)**
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL for the feedback baselines]** Godbole et al., arXiv 17 Sep 2019 (a search summary lists EMNLP 2019). HotpotQA full-wiki, 5.23M paragraphs; success means all supporting paragraphs are in the top k. BM25 accuracy@2/@5/@10/@20 = 0.093/0.191/0.259/0.324, MAP 0.412. Rocchio on TF-IDF (PRF-TFIDF) = 0.088/0.157/0.204/0.258, MAP 0.317. RM3 (PRF-RM) = 0.083/0.175/0.242/0.296, MAP 0.406. A BERT re-ranker reached 0.347 at @10 and the paper's entity-centric hop model 0.612. The authors' explanation: feedback relies on term frequency in the top documents and fails to use entities that may not be frequent in the paragraph. [Godbole et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.07598)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL for BM25, RM3 and 2-step rows]** SPRIG (Qizhi Wang, PingCAP; arXiv 2602.23372, PDF stamped 27 Dec 2025). HotpotQA validation, 7,405 queries, 66,581 passages: BM25 R@5 0.631, R@10 0.742, MRR 0.784; BM25+RM3 0.531/0.675/0.662; BM25 with two-step entity-based query expansion ("BM25-2step") 0.617/0.729/0.732. Paired bootstrap delta R@10 against BM25: RM3 -0.0671 [-0.0720, -0.0624], wins/ties/losses 227/5998/1180; BM25-2step -0.0137 [-0.0198, -0.0080], 808/5610/987. On 2WikiMultiHopQA (10,000 queries) RM3 -0.0583 [-0.0621, -0.0547] and BM25-2step +0.0094 [0.0048, 0.0139]. RM3 also tripled median latency (0.169 s against 0.057 s on HotpotQA). The paper concludes that "classic PRF is not consistently reliable for multi-hop retrieval". Quality caveats: single author; the 2Wiki BM25 R@10 is 0.643 in the main table but 0.512 in the robustness table, and the reference list carries wrong author names, so treat the 2Wiki figures as weak. [SPRIG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23372)
- **[MEASURED]** Re:CAP (Joshi et al., JPMorgan; arXiv 2609.24122 v2, 22 Sep 2026) used RM3 (10 feedback documents, 20 terms, original-query weight 0.5) as its classical control. Across six dataset by retriever cells (n = 98 to 100 each) "RM3 lifts top-500 recall by at most 1.6 pp over flat BM25 top-500". At a matched budget of about 240 documents RM3 reached 0.636 on MuSiQue (flat BM25 0.610 [0.558, 0.664]) and 0.832 on HotpotQA (flat BM25 0.827 [0.775, 0.878]). Bo1 feedback tracked RM3 within 1 point. Re:CAP itself, an LLM loop of 250 to 500 calls per audited query **[LLM-Q]**, reached 0.901 [0.854, 0.938] on MuSiQue at 238 documents, and its leave-one-out table attributes the lift to an entity ledger: dropping it costs 8.58 points on MuSiQue and 4.17 on HotpotQA, and changes MultiHop-RAG by -1.47 (the lever hurt slightly there). [Re:CAP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24122)
- **[CLASSIC] [MEASURED]** Collins-Thompson, CIKM 2009. Relevance-model expansion (Indri 2.2) against no expansion on six TREC sets: MAP +31.9% (TREC 1&2), +13.8% (TREC 7), +15.5% (TREC 8), +5.2% (wt10g), +13.5% (robust2004), +6.5% (gov2). Robustness index, defined as (queries helped minus queries hurt) divided by all queries: 0.4844, 0.4146, 0.4286, -0.0270, 0.3364, 0.0922. On wt10g more queries were hurt than helped even though mean MAP rose. The paper's R-Loss@20 measure (net loss of relevant documents in the top 20 due to expansion failures), as tabulated: 117/366, 23/57, 29/76, 59/158, 124/394, 171/575. A risk-constrained selection of expansion terms cut that loss by 21.0% to 76.9%, kept MAP within one point of the baseline expansion or above it (TREC 8 was 14.5% against 15.5%), and raised the wt10g index to 0.2703. [Collins-Thompson 2009](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~kevynct/pubs/ir0537-collinsthompson.pdf)
- **[MEASURED]** Liang, McDonald and Ounis (workshop extended abstract at SIGIR 2026, arXiv 13 Jul 2026): in an audit with 108 users over 43 TREC DL 2019 queries, 20.9% of queries benefited from feedback and 25.6% were degraded; avoiding harm was valued nearly twice as much as exploiting a successful expansion. [Liang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12098)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL]** For contrast on single-hop document sets, a sibling note records RM3 at 38.7 against 36.5 nDCG@20 for plain BM25 on 13 low-resource BEIR sets (Mar 2026); not re-fetched here. [Jedidi and Lin 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11008)

**Feedback and lexical query handling measured on conversational memory**
- **[MEASURED] [NER, RERANK]** SmartSearch (Derehag et al., Midbrain; arXiv 16 Mar 2026) keeps raw conversation text with no ingest step, recalls by exact substring match with part-of-speech term weights, and reranks with a cross-encoder plus ColBERT. Its feedback pass adds nouns and proper nouns that occur in at least 2 of the top 10 passages, weight 0.5, one extra pass. Its rule-based entity discovery adds person, organisation, place and event names found in retrieved passages but absent from the query, weight 2.5, for the next hop. LoCoMo (baseline 91.0): feedback -0.1, entity discovery +0.1, both -0.3. LongMemEval-S (baseline 78.4): feedback +6.0, entity discovery +1.4, both +9.2, with temporal questions rising from 67.7 to 80.5. Morphological expansion was tested and rejected. Oracle hop distribution on LoCoMo: 97.0% single-hop, 2.8% two-hop, 0.2% three-hop. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL]** open-memex retrieval ablation (practitioner study dated 7 Oct 2026; LongMemEval-S cleaned, 470 non-abstention questions, session-level, SQLite FTS5 BM25, retrieval only). Baseline R@1 86.0%, R@5 97.0%, R@10 98.3%, MRR 0.909. Switching term matching from OR to AND collapsed R@5 to 26.0% and MRR to 0.257. Removing prefix matching raised MRR to 0.919, so prefix matching was mildly harmful. Removing stopword filtering gave 0.904. A second-chance synonym round fired on 0 of 470 queries at limits up to 20 and rescued nothing. MRR by type: knowledge-update 0.981, multi-session 0.929, temporal-reasoning 0.851, single-session-preference 0.652. Pooling all 22,419 sessions into one corpus dropped R@5 to 40.6%. [open-memex ablation](https://unpkg.com/open-memex@0.8.1/docs/retrieval-ablation-study.md)
- **[SNIPPET] [NO MODEL]** Other BM25-only session recall reports on LongMemEval-S: agentmemory 86.5% R@5 and 96.2% R@10 on the 133 multi-session questions using recall-any; pgmnemo recall@10 0.982 for BM25 against 0.933 for bge-m3 on n = 500. These count a hit when any gold session is returned, so they say nothing about complete evidence coverage. [agentmemory](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/rohitg00/agentmemory@main/benchmark/LONGMEMEVAL.md); [pgmnemo](https://pgxn.org/dist/pgmnemo/docs/BENCHMARKS.html)
- **[MEASURED]** Lysenstøen (arXiv 2 Jun 2026): training-free fusion of BM25 with late-interaction scores gave +8.8 to +17.2 Hit@1 over late interaction alone on LoCoMo and +11.2 over BM25 at best (Hit@1 0.752); on LongMemEval-S, where BM25 is already strong, the gain over BM25 was small and not significant, and a web-search cross-encoder applied to the fused top 10 lowered Hit@1 by 6.9. [Lysenstøen 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04194)
- **[MEASURED]** SeCom (Pan et al., arXiv 8 Feb 2025): on LoCoMo BM25 beat MPNet at turn level (GPT4Score 65.58 against 57.99) and session level (63.16 against 51.18). [SeCom](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05589v1)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-Q]** LongMemEval paper (Wu et al., ICLR 2025): most questions need several sessions, up to six. LLM-written time-aware query expansion raised Recall@5 on temporal questions from 0.639 to 0.654 (sessions) and 0.421 to 0.451 (rounds) with GPT-4o, and lowered it to 0.624 and 0.384 with Llama 3.1 8B. [LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2)

**Decomposition, routing and joins**
- **[CLASSIC] [NO MODEL]** Region algebra (Clarke, Cormack and Burkowski, The Computer Journal 1995): queries return sets of text intervals; seven operators combine intervals (containing, not containing, contained in, not contained in, one of, both of, followed by); cost is at worst proportional to the access cost of the elementary term occurrences. The same group's shortest-substring and cover density ranking scores a document by the shortest intervals that cover the query terms. No use on conversational memory was found. [Clarke et al. 1995](https://uwaterloo.ca/data-systems-group/publications/algebra-structured-text-search-and-framework-its); [Clarke thesis](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~claclark/phd.pdf)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-Q]** LOTUS semantic join: the reference algorithm is a nested loop with one LLM call per pair; on BioDEX the optimised join plus rank used 5,869 LLM calls for rank-precision@5 0.265, against an estimated 6,092,500 calls for the naive plan. This is the database notion of a join over text, and it is LLM-bound. [LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11418)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, LLM-Q in part]** PRISM (Peng et al., arXiv 12 May 2026) routes by intent with keyword regexes first: 32.2% of LoCoMo queries were settled by keywords and 82.6% of temporal queries matched keyword triggers. Its typed-path graph search and intent-sensitive edge costs made no measurable difference on LoCoMo. Its annotation of LoCoMo: 73.4% of category 1 to 4 questions cite a single evidence entry, and about 6% (95% interval 2.1 to 16.5%, 50 sampled questions) need an explicit two-hop bridge. [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12260v1)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, EMB]** Eywa (Joshi, arXiv 29 May 2026) has an LLM-free read path: a hand-coded planner classifies query shape (exact, recommendation, inference, count, interval, boolean, list, factoid) and sets channel weights over vector, SQLite FTS5 BM25, a validity-window date filter and entity graph traversal, fused by weighted reciprocal rank with k = 20. LongMemEval-S 88.2% overall, multi-session 84.2%, temporal 87.2% with GPT-4o. Of 59 remaining failures: 27 fact not retrieved, 12 candidate lost before the final context, 12 fact not extracted, 7 assembly across multiple memories, 1 packed evidence unused. No ablation of the planner is reported. [Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771)
- **[MEASURED] [NER]** EdgeMem (arXiv 3 Sep 2026) uses two deterministic query triggers: a fixed lexical trigger that raises the number of distinct sessions sampled from 3 to 5 for enumerative questions, and a date gate that opens the time channel only when the query carries an explicit month and four-digit year. The enumerative trigger is not ablated on its own; the gated time channel as a whole adds +0.58 strict-judge points on LoCoMo (details in the index section). [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)

### Inferences
- The failure of RM3 on bridge questions is structural. Feedback terms are the frequent terms of the first-hop documents, so they reinforce the first hop; the second hop is reached through a rare entity. Godbole, SPRIG and the Re:CAP entity-ledger ablation all point the same way.
- In a single user's conversation the role of feedback changes from bridging to vocabulary repair, which fits the pattern "neutral on LoCoMo, +6.0 on the 115k-token LongMemEval-S haystack". The published gain was obtained with a strong neural reranker downstream; whether it survives in a purely lexical pipeline is untested.
- "What did we try for X" and "how did the plan change" are closer to a group-by on a key followed by an order-by on time than to bridge hopping. The relevant database operation is a semi-join of the span table against the set of keys that co-occur with X, not an iterative hop.
- LoCoMo is a weak testbed for bridge-style mechanisms: two independent audits put true multi-hop at about 3% to 6% of its questions. LongMemEval multi-session (133 questions) and temporal (133) are the smallest credible targets.
- Any feedback lane should be reported with per-question wins, ties and losses and a robustness index, since mean gains hide the harmed share (Collins-Thompson; Liang et al.).

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of RM3, Rocchio and entity expansion on LongMemEval or LoCoMo in a pipeline without a neural reranker was found.
- No study of selective (gated) feedback on conversational memory was found.
- No model-free query decomposition method with measurements was found; published decomposition for memory uses an LLM.
- No measured application of interval joins, region algebra or cover density to conversation spans was found.
- The hop distribution of LongMemEval questions is not published in the sources read (SmartSearch states it has oracle traces for LoCoMo only).

## Materialized views, incremental view maintenance, differential dataflow and semantic operators (LOTUS, DocETL, Palimpzest, TAG, text-to-SQL over memory): are precomputed views over conversation history maintained incrementally and deterministically, and what was measured?

### Takeaway
No work was found that maintains deterministic, incrementally updated views (per topic, per entity, timeline, decision log) over a verbatim conversation log and measures their retrieval value in isolation. The nearest systems are EdgeMem (append-only hubs, measured), DMF (deterministic cards, no ablation), Bitacorista (regex decision log, no measurement), Stashbird (provenance-counted reconciliation with LLM extraction, measured for consistency) and AutoViewMem (LLM-induced views, measured), while the semantic-operator engines are LLM-per-tuple systems whose papers, as far as read, do not address view maintenance.

### Cited Findings

**Classic machinery**
- **[CLASSIC]** DBSP (Budiu, Chajed, McSherry, Ryzhyk, Tannen; PVLDB 16(7), 2023, best paper at VLDB 2023; arXiv Mar 2022) defines the incremental form of a query as differentiation, the lifted query, then integration over streams of changes. For linear operators the work is proportional to the size of the change. It covers relational algebra, nested relations, aggregation and recursion, and states that it began as a simpler formalisation of Differential Dataflow (McSherry et al., CIDR 2013). [DBSP](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p1601-budiu.pdf); [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16684v1)
- **[CLASSIC]** Gupta, Mumick and Subrahmanian (SIGMOD 1993): the counting algorithm keeps, for each derived tuple, the number of alternative derivations, so a deletion removes a tuple only when its count reaches zero; the delete-and-rederive algorithm handles recursive views. [Gupta et al. 1993](https://researchr.org/publication/GuptaMS93)
- **[MEASURED]** Enzyme (Databricks; SIGMOD Companion 2026, arXiv 29 Mar 2026) is a production view-maintenance engine. A cost model picks incremental or full refresh; on TPC-DI it incrementalised 100% of 8 datasets, 6 of 8 with substantial speedups, and the cost model chose correctly in 7 of 8 cases. One dataset was slower incrementally because more than 95% of rows are rewritten per batch. It mentions "query mutators" for current_timestamp() and AI functions without describing the AI case; non-deterministic user functions fall back to full recomputation. [Enzyme](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27775v1)
- **[VENDOR] [PROPOSED]** Pathway's engine is built on Differential Dataflow and keeps a live retrieval index in step with added, changed and deleted documents; RisingWave and Materialize propose materialized views over conversation history for preference summaries and session statistics. No independent benchmark was found for any of the three. [Pathway](https://pathway.com/developers/showcases/llm-app-pathway); [RisingWave](https://risingwave.com/blog/ai-agent-memory-streaming-database/); [Materialize](https://materialize.com/blog/agents-need-digital-twins/)

**Semantic operators, table-augmented generation and text-to-SQL**
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-Q]** LOTUS (Patel et al., arXiv v3 1 Mar 2025; PVLDB 18) defines sem_filter, sem_join, sem_topk, sem_agg, sem_group_by, sem_map, sem_extract, sem_search, sem_sim_join and sem_index, with accuracy targets relative to a reference algorithm. FEVER, 1,000 claims: 91.0% accuracy in 190.0 s batched, against FacTool 80.9% in 5,396.11 s. SciFact nDCG@10 0.765 against 0.741 for a reranker. The paper does not discuss incremental maintenance, materialized views or result caching, and has no conversational evaluation. [LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11418); [PVLDB copy](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol18/p4171-patel.pdf)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-Q]** DocETL (Shankar et al., arXiv v3 1 Apr 2025; PVLDB 18) adds Split, Gather ("augments each chunk with context from surrounding chunks") and Resolve (entity resolution) to map, reduce, filter and equijoin, with 13 rewrite directives. CUAD contract extraction, first 50 contracts: optimised F1 0.477, unoptimised 0.379, LOTUS 0.393, Palimpzest 0.022. It caches sub-pipeline outputs; incremental processing is not discussed; Split records document id and chunk number, which is lineage in all but name. [DocETL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- **[SNIPPET] [LLM-Q]** Palimpzest (Liu et al., CIDR 2025): plans up to 3.3x faster and 2.9x cheaper than a baseline in a single-thread setting with better F1. SemBench (Lao et al., arXiv 3 Nov 2025, accepted to VLDB 2026) benchmarks LOTUS, Palimpzest, ThalamusDB and BigQuery across text, image and audio and calls its results a snapshot. [Palimpzest](https://vldb.org/cidrdb/2025/palimpzest-optimizing-ai-powered-analytics-with-declarative-query-processing.html); [SemBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01716)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-Q]** TAG (Biswal et al., arXiv 27 Aug 2024): 80 queries adapted from BIRD needing world knowledge or reasoning. Exact match: Text2SQL 0.17, retrieval-augmented generation 0.00, retrieval plus LLM rank 0.02, Text2SQL plus LLM 0.13, hand-written TAG pipelines on LOTUS 0.55. Aggregation queries were not scored. The winning pipelines were written by hand. [TAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14717v1)
- **[MEASURED] [VENDOR] [LLM-W]** "From Unstructured Recall to Schema-Grounded Memory" (xmemory; arXiv v3 13 Aug 2026) argues that exact facts, aggregation, state tracking and relational queries need addressable records and names text-to-SQL as a practical read interface. On the authors' own benchmark F1 was 97.10% against Mem0 87.24%, Cognee 86.18% and Zep 80.16%; on a Splitwise-style task 95.2% against Supermemory 73.75%, Mem0 54.9% and Zep 25.7%. No BM25 baseline; the authors say thematic and exploratory recall still favour unstructured retrieval. Materialized views are not mentioned. [xmemory paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27906)
- **[SNIPPET]** The reverse direction also exists: AgentSM (Jan 2026) stores execution traces as structured memory for text-to-SQL agents and reports 25% fewer tokens and 35% shorter trajectories on Spider 2.0. [AgentSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15709v1)

**View-like structures over conversation history**
- **[MEASURED] [NER] [NO LLM, NO EMB]** EdgeMem: construction is append-only; each incoming turn "contributes one stable source record and links it to its time, co-occurrence, and episode hubs". The hubs are month buckets, one posting list per entity or lemma, and one per session. Build cost for LoCoMo: 0 LLM tokens and 1.8 minutes single-threaded, against 0.46M to 11.77M tokens and 17 minutes to 10.1 hours for six reproduced LLM-built systems. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED] [EMB] [NO LLM in memory management]** DMF (Stabile and Zimuel, Roma Tre; arXiv 2 Jun 2026) archives raw records and derives "memory cards" with kind, subject, predicate, object, qualifiers, time anchor, validity and a source record id; cards are "retrieval aids, not replacements for raw records" and are expanded to raw turns before rendering. Decay is by interaction count, so memory state is a function of the message sequence alone; a supersession penalty of 0.35 demotes replaced entries. LoCoMo judge score 0.7753 against Mem0 0.6883 (n = 1,540): temporal 0.7072 against 0.1526, multi-hop tied at 0.7943, single-hop lower (0.8181 against 0.8716). On a 60-question LongMemEval sample Mem0 led 0.767 to 0.717, and 0.700 to 0.500 on multi-session. Zero memory tokens against 49.98M for Mem0 on LoCoMo. No ablation; only "narrow, deterministically normalisable patterns" become cards. [DMF](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03463)
- **[PROPOSED] [NO MODEL]** Bitacorista (PyPI package, v2.9.0 uploaded 14 Apr 2026) detects decisions, discards, corrections, topic changes, deliverables, discoveries and questions with bilingual regexes and negation filtering, stores them in SQLite, and marks entries FIRME, INFERIDO, DESCARTADO or OBSOLETO ("replaced by a newer decision"), with supersession edges and a version-chain command in project mode. No accuracy or benchmark figures; the author states it handles decisions spread over several messages poorly. [Bitacorista](https://pypi.org/project/bitacorista/)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, LLM-Q]** Stashbird (Biringa et al., Microsoft; arXiv v2 17 Oct 2026) keeps for every derived entity and relation the set of supporting episodes. Repeated evidence extends support, a correction supersedes the active relation and keeps the old version, and deleting an episode removes it from each support set and drops only relations whose support becomes empty. With reconciliation the final state was correct in 20 of 20 update sequences, against 8 of 20 without; all checks passed across 263 deletion interventions, where deleting every affected record would have lost 441 still-supported relation occurrences on LoCoMo. LongMemEval-S 81.0 +/- 0.8 against Hindsight 78.3 +/- 2.6 and Mem0 70.0 +/- 0.6 (GPT-4.1-mini); LoCoMo 87.5 +/- 0.3 against Hindsight 89.1 +/- 0.7. Ingestion used 484K prompt tokens against 37M for Graphiti. It cites database provenance work, not view maintenance. [Stashbird](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34242v2)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, EMB]** AutoViewMem (Cao et al., arXiv 18 Sep 2026): a "view" is an LLM-proposed schema with a name, slots and an extraction template; every 50 chunks an LLM proposes 10 candidates, a determinantal point process keeps 10 low-overlap views, and an LLM extracts items per view with provenance span ids. LoCoMo, Qwen3-8B: overall judge 0.837 against MemGAS 0.783, A-mem 0.720, Mem0 0.683 and full history 0.821. Ablation: single-view structured extraction 0.774, random view choice 0.777, no consolidation 0.801; share of queries with no gold evidence in the retrieved pool 5.14% (full), 5.53%, 7.03%, 9.83%. No comparison with BM25 over raw turns. [AutoViewMem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21940)
- **[PROPOSED]** Eywa keeps SQLite as the authority for evidence, facts and provenance and treats vector indexes and graph checkpoints as "idempotent projections" rebuildable from it; it has no atomic cross-store transaction. ESAA (26 Feb 2026) has agents emit structured intentions that a deterministic orchestrator appends to a log and "projects a verifiable materialized view", verified by replay hashing in two case studies (49 and 86 events). [Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771); [ESAA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23193)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W]** A caution on approximate maintenance: Zep's community summaries are updated by label propagation that "gradually diverges from full recomputation, so periodic refreshes are needed". [Zep](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)

### Inferences
- TAG's 0.00 exact match for plain retrieval on match, comparison and ranking questions over tables (aggregation was left unscored) is the database-side statement of the problem: set-level questions are not answerable from a top-k sample. "What did we try for X" is an aggregation over the event set of X, and "how did the plan change" is an ordered scan of it. A view keyed by X turns both into point lookups.
- With immutable, append-only spans the maintenance problem is the easy case of the classic theory: only insertions occur, the candidate views (postings per key, per-key timeline, per-key value sequence, decision log) are monotone in the log, and each new turn touches only the keys it mentions. Support counting in the Stashbird or Gupta sense is needed only if spans can be retracted.
- A view that is a pure function of the log is reproducible and can be rebuilt or audited by replay, which fits a deterministic prompt builder and is the property DMF and Eywa both claim.
- The measurable question is whether a deterministic view lookup raises complete-evidence coverage at a fixed token budget over the same lanes without the view. None of the systems above reports that isolation.
- Zep's drift note argues for exact incremental rules over heuristic ones wherever a derived structure feeds the prompt.

### Gaps
- No deterministic, incrementally maintained conversation view with an isolated ablation was found.
- No evaluation of LOTUS, DocETL or Palimpzest on conversational memory was found. The LOTUS paper and the readable part of the DocETL paper do not discuss view maintenance; the DocETL page was truncated after its first evaluation section and the Palimpzest paper was not read in full.
- Enzyme does not explain how AI functions are incrementalised.
- The vendor proposals (RisingWave, Materialize, Pathway) come without independent measurements.
- The xmemory page was truncated before its LoCoMo and LongMemEval appendix; those results are unknown here.
- Bitacorista's regex detection has no published precision or recall.

## Index structures: inverted indexes with positional or proximity scoring, zone maps or block-max indexes, skip lists, learned indexes, suffix arrays or FM-indexes, bitmap indexes over entities or values, temporal and bitemporal indexes for "what was true when", provenance semirings and lineage

### Takeaway
Proximity scoring is the best-evidenced classic signal the target system lacks (about +1.7 NDCG@10 over BM25 on a web test set, with mixed results noted in the same literature), while speed-oriented structures matter little at single-conversation scale. For conversational memory the only index work found concerns time partitioning for latency and bitemporal stores whose quality gains are entangled with LLM extraction; no work was found on block-max or zone maps for quality, learned indexes, FM-indexes or bitmap indexes, and provenance semirings have one position paper with preliminary numbers.

### Cited Findings

**Positional and proximity scoring**
- **[CLASSIC] [SNIPPET]** Tao and Zhai (SIGIR 2007) compared five proximity measures and found minimum pair distance the most useful; adding it to BM25 and to the KL-divergence language model improved retrieval on five TREC collections. The primary PDF could not be reached, so no numbers are given. [secondary review](https://liner.com/review/exploration-proximity-measures-in-information-retrieval)
- **[CLASSIC] [MEASURED]** Svore, Kanani and Khan (SIGIR 2010), web test set of 11,857 queries, NDCG@1/@3/@10: BM25 24.60/27.74/34.34; BM25 plus a pairwise proximity term (adjacent and non-adjacent query bigrams) 26.06/29.54/36.00; adjacent bigrams only 25.27/28.72/35.35. On tail queries BM25 scored 21.23/25.13/32.05 and the proximity variant 23.21/28.73/36.04. The paper's review of earlier work records caveats: one bigram-proximity study had "almost negligible results on MAP", span-based scoring gave "mixed results" on the web, proximity features "can be neutral or even negative" when the base ranker is already strong, and with proximity features query length was inversely correlated with effectiveness. [Svore et al. 2010](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=936)
- **[MEASURED] [NER]** A two-field form of lexical scoring already exists for conversations: EdgeMem mixes turn-level and session-level BM25 with weight 0.5 each, and reads each seed turn with its predecessor and successor. That step moved the LoCoMo strict-judge score from 49.74 to 58.18. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)

**Block-max, zone maps, pruning, time partitions**
- **[CLASSIC]** Ding and Suel (SIGIR 2011): a block-max index stores the maximum score of each compressed posting block so whole blocks can be skipped, with results identical to exhaustive scoring. Lucene's adoption made term queries 3x to 7x faster and disjunctions between 8% slower and 15x faster on its benchmark suite. [Ding and Suel 2011](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~suel/papers/bmw.pdf); [Elastic blog](https://www.elastic.co/blog/faster-retrieval-of-top-hits-in-elasticsearch-with-block-max-wand)
- **[CLASSIC] [SNIPPET]** Small Materialized Aggregates (Moerkotte, VLDB 1998) precompute per-bucket aggregates such as minimum and maximum, are cheap to maintain under bulk loads, and gave about 100x on TPC-D query 1 according to a third-party summary. [Moerkotte 1998](https://www.vldb.org/conf/1998/p476.pdf)
- **[SNIPPET] [MEASURED]** AgentIR (Yuan, Zhang et al., arXiv 2605.25092, May 2026; read through a third-party review, not the paper): 7-day time partitions searched recent-first, MaxScore pruning, and a cascade that runs BM25 first (about 0.4 ms) and skips the dense channel (52 ms) when the confidence (s0 - s1)/s0 passes a threshold. On LongMemEval the cascade skipped 63% of queries and was 2.67x faster (19.9 ms against 53.2 ms) at equal judged accuracy; on LoCoMo it skipped 100% of queries. Absolute judged accuracy was low (0.254 hybrid), so this is speed evidence only. [AgentIR review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/agentir-a-workload-adaptive-cascade-retrieval-substrate-for-long-term-conversational-memory)
- **[MEASURED]** SwiftMem (Tian et al., arXiv 13 Jan 2026): a temporal index with logarithmic-time range queries plus a tag hierarchy; search latency 10.8 ms on LoCoMo and 12.7 ms on LongMemEval-S with accuracy described as competitive. [SwiftMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08160)
- **[MEASURED]** Scale check: SmartSearch scans the raw conversation by substring with no index, and that scan resolved 98.9% of LoCoMo oracle traces; its 650 ms per query is dominated by rerankers. EdgeMem retrieval runs at 10.73 ms at the 95th percentile on a desktop CPU. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1); [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)

**Learned indexes, suffix arrays and FM-indexes, bitmaps and value postings**
- **[CLASSIC]** Learned indexes (Kraska et al., arXiv 4 Dec 2017) outperformed cache-optimised B-Trees "by up to 70% in speed" with about an order of magnitude less memory, and frame a bitmap index as a model of whether a record exists. [Kraska et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01208)
- **[MEASURED]** Infini-gram mini (Xu et al., EMNLP 2025 best paper): an FM-index at 44% of corpus size over 46TB of text with query time independent of corpus size; used for benchmark contamination checks, not memory. [infini-gram mini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12229)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL for the detectors]** Typed value detection exists in Eywa: deterministic detectors for dates, entities, monetary values, versions, identifiers, URLs, IP addresses, percentages and quoted strings, stored append-only. Such hard anchors were sparse on chat data: 59 of 2,541 persisted facts in its main LoCoMo store carried one, and 67.4% of candidate facts in a 143-sample audit had none. [Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771)

**Temporal and bitemporal indexes**
- **[CLASSIC]** SQL:2011 (Kulkarni and Michels, SIGMOD Record 2012) separates system-versioned tables, which record when the database knew a row, from application-time period tables, which record when a row was true in the world; a table with both is bitemporal. [Kulkarni and Michels 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2380776.2380786); [SQL:2011 overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL:2011)
- **[MEASURED] [VENDOR] [LLM-W]** Zep and Graphiti (Rasmussen et al., arXiv Jan 2025) put four timestamps on each fact edge (created and expired on the transaction timeline, valid and invalid on the event timeline); an LLM detects contradictions and sets the older edge's invalid time. Raw episodes are kept and linked both ways to derived facts, but those links were not evaluated. LongMemEval-S: 71.2% against 60.2% for full context with gpt-4o and 63.8% against 55.4% with gpt-4o-mini; with gpt-4o temporal reasoning 45.1 to 62.4, multi-session 44.3 to 57.9, knowledge update 78.2 to 83.3, single-session-assistant 94.6 to 80.4. Later papers place Zep at 63.8 on LongMemEval-S against 88.4 for SmartSearch and at 63.2 in Stashbird's table. [Zep](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2501.13956); [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1); [Stashbird](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34242v2)
- **[MEASURED] [EMB]** Niksarli and Baheti (arXiv 29 Jul 2026): immutable identity nodes with version nodes carrying valid-time and transaction-time intervals in Neo4j. On 60 sampled LongMemEval questions R@10 was 46.7% overall and 80% on knowledge-update; using the time-travel path, recall on temporal-reasoning questions fell from 50% to 37.5%, which the authors attribute to post-filter dilution. No baseline. [Niksarli and Baheti 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26520)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL]** Cheap time signals: EdgeMem's month hubs with the explicit-date gate added +0.58 [0.07, 1.15] strict-judge points on LoCoMo (+0.45 when used alone on flat BM25). In SYNAPSE, switching off temporal decay dropped LoCoMo temporal F1 from 50.1 to 14.2. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553); [SYNAPSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02744v3)

**Provenance semirings and lineage**
- **[CLASSIC]** Green, Karvounarakis and Tannen (PODS 2007): annotate tuples with variables and propagate polynomials through queries; bag semantics, why-provenance, probabilistic and incomplete databases are instances obtained by evaluating the polynomial in different semirings. [Green et al. 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)
- **[MEASURED, preliminary] [PROPOSED for the rest]** Asma, Hernández and Flouris (workshop at ISWC, 25 to 26 Oct 2026; position paper) attach how-provenance polynomials to retrieved facts. Across three open-weight models, three prompt phrasings and sixteen query sets, a bare provenance token cut acceptance of fabricated facts by roughly 60% relative to an undifferentiated fact list at a recall cost of a few points, and beat a flat source-lineage baseline. Substituting trust values into the polynomial is left to future work. [Asma et al. 2026](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4313/glow-paper12.pdf)
- **[MEASURED]** LineageRAG (Zheng et al., arXiv 17 Aug 2026) builds one evidence lineage per evidence demand of the query and completes each with a verbatim source span; average gains over leading GraphRAG baselines on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue were +3.51 R@5, +5.96 EM, +5.22 F1. The abstract does not say whether an LLM is in the retrieval loop. [LineageRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16004)
- **[MEASURED]** Lineage used for evaluation: AutoViewMem maps retrieved items to source span ids before scoring so that several view-specific items from one span do not inflate coverage. Lineage used for maintenance: Stashbird's support sets, above. [AutoViewMem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21940)

### Inferences
- At 10^5 to 10^6 tokens per conversation, block-max, skip lists and learned indexes solve a problem the system does not have. The structures worth adding are the ones that carry a new ranking or filtering signal: positions, zones (span against session), sorted time, and typed value postings.
- Inside paragraph-sized spans proximity is likely to add little, in line with the literature's "neutral when the base ranker is strong" caveat. The untested variant is proximity across adjacent spans: the shortest run of consecutive spans that covers the query terms, which is cover density lifted from words to spans and would return contiguous evidence windows.
- A zone-map analogue for quality is a per-session summary row (first and last turn, term maxima, value types present) used to guarantee that every session able to contribute is at least considered. AgentIR uses the same partitioning only for speed.
- A model-free approximation of valid time is available from the value lane: for a key with a sequence of asserted values, the interval of each value runs from its assertion turn to the next different value for the same key. Transaction time is the turn index and is always exact. Eywa's sparsity figure warns that typed anchors cover a small share of chat-style facts; coverage should be higher in technical logs, which is an assumption to test.
- The bitemporal results found do not isolate the index from LLM extraction, and one shows time filtering lowering recall. Time should be a soft channel or a gated one, as in EdgeMem, not a hard post-filter.
- Provenance polynomials give a principled form for lane fusion: record for each packed span which lanes and query terms derived it, then score under a counting semiring (number of independent derivations). This is a proposal; nothing measured was found.

### Gaps
- Tao and Zhai's per-collection numbers were not retrieved.
- The AgentIR paper itself was not read; its figures come from a review page.
- No work was found on block-max or zone maps used for retrieval quality, on learned indexes, on suffix arrays or FM-indexes, or on compressed bitmap indexes for conversational memory.
- No ablation was found that separates bitemporal modelling from LLM fact extraction.
- No measurement of provenance-based ranking or packing was found.

## Graph and link methods built without a model: co-occurrence graphs, shared-entity links, personalized PageRank or spreading activation over passages (HippoRAG-style but model-free, LinearRAG, EdgeMem and similar), and evidence on when chronology or adjacency explains the gain

### Takeaway
Graphs built from named-entity or keyword co-occurrence with no LLM are now established (LinearRAG, SPRIG, KET-RAG's keyword layer, EdgeMem, Zero-Mem), but all except EdgeMem and one SPRIG variant need embeddings at query time. On conversational data the published ablations assign most of the gain to session and neighbour context and to time, with co-occurrence links adding about 2 points on top and graph path search adding nothing on LoCoMo, whereas on Wikipedia-style multi-hop the entity graph matters more than the hierarchy.

### Cited Findings

**Model-free or LLM-free graph construction on document benchmarks**
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W]** HippoRAG (Gutiérrez et al., NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 23 May 2024): LLM-built knowledge graph plus personalized PageRank; "up to 20%" over prior retrieval methods on multi-hop question answering, 10 to 30 times cheaper and 6 to 13 times faster than iterative retrieval. This is the design the model-free variants strip down. [HippoRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831)
- **[MEASURED] [NER, EMB]** LinearRAG (Zhuang et al., arXiv Oct 2025; ICLR 2026): a graph of passages, sentences and entities from spaCy with no relation extraction, zero LLM tokens for indexing and retrieval, entity activation through sentence similarity, then personalized PageRank (damping 0.85). Containment and GPT-judged accuracy with a GPT-4o-mini reader: HotpotQA 64.30/66.50 against HippoRAG2 62.90/64.30 and plain top-5 retrieval 55.70/58.60; 2Wiki 70.20/63.70 against HippoRAG 66.10/59.90 and plain 48.60/43.00; MuSiQue 33.90/37.00 against HippoRAG2 31.00/35.00 and plain 26.10/29.60. Indexing 2Wiki took 249.78 s against 1,147.01 s for HippoRAG2. No BM25 baseline; ablations are shown only as charts. [LinearRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10114v4); [ICLR listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007603)
- **[MEASURED] [NER or regex, NO EMB in the hybrid variant]** SPRIG: an entity to passage bipartite graph weighted by TF-IDF, personalized PageRank with restart 0.15 and 5 iterations. Seeding from query entities alone was far worse than BM25 (HotpotQA R@10 0.464 against 0.742). Seeding from the top BM25 passages (10 on HotpotQA, 5 on 2Wiki; the "GraphHybrid" variant) gave 0.775 on HotpotQA, delta +0.0330 [0.0284, 0.0369], wins/ties/losses 739/6411/255; on 2Wiki +0.0998 [0.0945, 0.1047]. A term-level TF-IDF graph with PageRank, their stand-in for MixPR, reached only 0.419 and 0.367. Pruning the top 1% of entities by document frequency removed 1.76% (HotpotQA) and 0.49% (2Wiki) of gold-title mentions and cut query time 16% to 28% with negligible recall change. A regex for capitalised spans cost about 0.02 ms per document against 9 to 10 ms for spaCy and gave similar hybrid recall (0.812 against 0.810 in the bucket where an entity matches a gold title); 6.7% of HotpotQA queries had no spaCy entity. Downstream answer gain with a small extractive reader was slight (exact match 0.173 to 0.180). Same quality caveats as in the first section. [SPRIG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23372)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL for the graph, LLM router]** MixPR (Alonso and Millidge, Zyphra; arXiv 8 Dec 2024): TF-IDF sentence vectors, a sparse sentence-similarity graph with chunks stored in chronological order, personalized PageRank with restart 0.6 for local questions and plain PageRank for global ones, CPU only. BABILong average 80.4 for GPT-4o with MixPR against 48.3 alone; RULER 96.5 against 95.3; HashHop at 100M tokens 96 against 90 for a fine-tuned long-context model. Baselines were TF-IDF nearest neighbour and a hybrid, not BM25, and graph structure was not ablated. This conflicts with SPRIG's weak result for a MixPR-like graph on open-corpus multi-hop; the tasks differ (one long document against a passage corpus). [MixPR](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06078v1)
- **[MEASURED] [EMB]** KET-RAG (Huang, Zhang, Xiao; KDD 2025): the keyword layer links every non-stop word to the sub-chunks containing it, with no LLM. In the low-cost setting it beat flat retrieval on MuSiQue (coverage 50.8 against 26.4, exact match 7.0 against 3.0) and HotpotQA (coverage 60.2 against 37.2); in the paper's high-accuracy setting (chunk length parameter 150 instead of 1,200) the gap nearly closed on MuSiQue (78.2 against 76.8) and stayed on HotpotQA (82.2 against 74.0), so part of the low-cost gain is a finer-granularity effect. [KET-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09304v2)

**Model-free structure on conversational memory**
- **[MEASURED] [NER] [NO LLM, NO EMB]** EdgeMem (Cui et al., PolyU and China Mobile; arXiv 3 Sep 2026): spaCy small model, rank_bm25 and dateparser only; verbatim turns as vertices; three hub families. Co-occurrence channel: query keys whose document frequency lies in a rare band of 2 to 10 select previously unseen turns that share the key. Pack limit 18 items and 3,072 tokens. LoCoMo strict-judge ladder with a shared answer prompt: flat turn BM25 49.74; plus session structure and neighbours 58.18 (+8.44 [5.79, 10.91]); plus co-occurrence 60.43 (+2.21 [0.76, 3.50]); plus time 61.01 (+0.58 [0.07, 1.15]). Used alone on flat BM25: co-occurrence +5.65, time +0.45. Reversing the insertion order gives +5.65 then +5.04; session and co-occurrence are "about 3.4 points short of additive". Evidence grew only from 727 to 944 tokens across the ladder. Against six reproduced systems under the same prompt: 61.01 against CompassMem 58.70 and CAM 54.94. LongMemEval-S session retrieval (n = 470): R@3 81.49 and R@10 93.83, against MemGAS 78.51 and 94.47, HippoRAG 2 75.53 and 91.28, Contriever 71.06 and 90.00 (baseline rows transcribed from another paper; no BM25 row). Its audit of 27 baseline failures lists "noise admitted by link expansion" among recurring causes. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED] [NER, EMB]** Zero-Mem (Xiao et al., PolyU; arXiv 31 Jul 2026): raw traces; an entity to context graph with co-occurrence edges and adjacency edges between neighbouring units; a hierarchy of turn, window, episode and local span; BM25 plus BGE-M3; personalized PageRank with damping 0.6; closure that adds neighbouring turns. LoCoMo F1 with GPT-4o-mini: 59.15 average against GAM 53.75, HippoRAG 47.92 and plain retrieval 44.73; on multi-hop it was not best (41.61 against 42.29). Ablation on HotpotQA in a 56K-token haystack: full 72.07 F1, graph only 62.50, hierarchy only 54.88, no closure 67.90. No ablation on LoCoMo. [Zero-Mem](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29377)
- **[MEASURED] [NER, RERANK]** SmartSearch's rule-based entity hop: +0.1 on LoCoMo, +1.4 on LongMemEval-S alone. Its reranker ladder moved LoCoMo from 76.8 with no reranker to 91.9, and the mean rank of the first gold passage among substring candidates from 195 to 8. The title claim is that ranking, not structure, carries conversational retrieval. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1)

**When chronology or adjacency explains the gain**
- **[MEASURED]** EdgeMem's largest step is the session and neighbour step (+8.44), nearly four times the incremental co-occurrence step (+2.21); the authors write that "most of what the memory contributes is the surrounding episode rather than the matched turn alone". The step bundles session-level BM25, distinct-session mixing and reading each seed with its two neighbours. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, EMB]** SYNAPSE (Jiang et al., arXiv v3 16 Feb 2026; a search summary lists Findings of ACL 2026), spreading activation over an LLM-extracted concept graph. LoCoMo F1 by category, full system against "no graph structure": multi-hop 35.7 against 35.2, temporal 50.1 against 25.4, average 40.5 against 32.9. Without temporal decay temporal F1 fell to 14.2. Removing the fan-effect normalisation (dividing by out-degree) cost multi-hop 5.5 points (30.2). The graph's measured value on LoCoMo is temporal. [SYNAPSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02744v3)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W, EMB]** "Does Memory Need Graphs?" (Hu et al., arXiv 3 Jan 2026), LongMemEval-S Recall@5: flat index over summaries, facts and keywords 0.9045; similarity-edge graph 0.8424; triple graph 0.9116; description graph 0.9331. One-hop expansion under the best re-ranking moved R@10 from 0.9904 to 0.9928, which the authors call marginal; under a weaker re-ranking it cut R@5 from 0.9546 to 0.8615. Raw sessions as keys gave R@5 0.9021. Temporal adjacency was listed but not tested. [Hu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01280v1)
- **[MEASURED]** PRISM: graph path search and intent-weighted edges were null on LoCoMo, consistent with its estimate that about 6% of questions need a bridge. [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12260v1)
- **[MEASURED] [EMB, RERANK]** Threader (Cai et al., Tsinghua; arXiv 27 Sep 2026) keeps raw interactions and groups them into topic segments with a small BERT classifier. Accuracy with 12 retrieved units (the figures match its LongMemEval results): segments 90.60, no segmentation 87.00, fixed chunks of size 500 85.60, single messages 85.80. [Threader](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33226)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W]** SeCom on LoCoMo with BM25: topical segments 71.57, turns 65.58, sessions 63.16 (GPT4Score; the segment row also includes a compression-based denoiser, whose removal cost 9.46 points with a dense retriever). LongMemEval paper with a dense retriever on its 500-session variant: session units Recall@5 0.706 against 0.582 for single rounds. [SeCom](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05589v1); [LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2)
- **[CLASSIC]** An old caution: Salton and Buckley (Information Processing and Management, 1988) summarised twenty years of evidence as "text indexing systems based on the assignment of appropriately weighted single terms produce retrieval results that are superior to those obtainable with other more elaborate text representations". Their separate SIGIR 1988 paper on spreading activation was located only bibliographically. [Salton and Buckley 1988](https://www.marilia.unesp.br/Home/Instituicao/Docentes/EdbertoFerneda/MRI%2004%20-%20Salton,%20G;%20Buckley,%20C%20-%201988.pdf); [SIGIR 1988 record](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/1988.sigirconf_conference-88.10)

### Inferences
- The bar a new link mechanism must clear on conversations is not flat BM25. It is flat BM25 plus neighbour reading plus distinct-session seeding, which already captures most of the published gain.
- Every model-free graph method that works applies a band-pass on document frequency: EdgeMem's 2 to 10 band, SPRIG's inverse-square-root hub penalty and top 1% pruning, SYNAPSE's fan effect. Keys that are too common connect everything, and keys seen once connect nothing.
- Query-entity seeding fails (SPRIG 0.464 against 0.742) and lexical seeding works. The transferable recipe is "first-pass lexical hits as seeds, then spread", which needs no embeddings.
- Personalized PageRank seeded by BM25 over a span to rare-key graph has not been measured on conversational data without embeddings. EdgeMem does a single hub lookup, and Zero-Mem and LinearRAG use dense similarity inside the propagation. The expected gain is modest (SPRIG +3.3 R@10 on HotpotQA) and link noise is a documented risk.
- Co-occurrence and adjacency partly substitute for each other (3.4 points of overlap), so their value must be judged by insertion-order ladders as EdgeMem does, not by single-component deltas.
- Entity graphs pay off where evidence lives in unrelated documents (Zero-Mem on HotpotQA: graph only 62.50 against hierarchy only 54.88). A conversation has a strong native order, so time and session carry more of the linkage.

### Gaps
- EdgeMem does not separate neighbour reading from session-level BM25 mixing and distinct-session sampling.
- No model-free personalized PageRank or spreading activation on conversational memory was found; SYNAPSE depends on LLM-extracted concepts and embeddings.
- No study compares a chronology-only expansion (seed plus k neighbours plus same session) with an entity-link expansion at equal token budget across LongMemEval question types.
- Zero-Mem has no LoCoMo ablation; LinearRAG has no numeric ablation and no BM25 baseline.
- The results of Salton and Buckley's spreading activation study were not retrieved.

## Result diversification and set selection for multi-aspect questions: xQuAD, PM-2, MMR, coverage-based or submodular selection, time-spread sampling, and evidence on multi-evidence recall

### Takeaway
In classic search, explicit-aspect methods are the ones that work (xQuAD up to +30% alpha-NDCG@5 over BM25; PM-2 0.4546 against xQuAD 0.4074 and MMR 0.3349 on the 2010 Web track) and implicit novelty (MMR) is the weakest and sometimes below no diversification. In retrieval-augmented generation MMR-style selection yields a few points when its weight is tuned per query, and on conversational memory the only direct data are a null result for MMR, a bundled distinct-session rule, and one report showing complete-evidence coverage trailing mean recall by about 8 points. No work was found applying xQuAD or PM-2 to context selection, or measuring time-spread sampling.

### Cited Findings

**Classic explicit-aspect diversification**
- **[CLASSIC] [MEASURED]** xQuAD (Santos, Macdonald and Ounis, WWW 2010) scores a document by a mixture of its relevance to the query and its relevance to query aspects (sub-queries) not yet covered. TREC 2009 Web track diversity task with official sub-topics as aspects, alpha-NDCG at 5/10/100: BM25 0.159/0.186/0.288; BM25 plus MMR 0.120/0.150/0.224; plus IA-Select 0.110/0.119/0.180; plus xQuAD 0.208/0.227/0.324, "gains of up to 30%". With aspects taken from search-engine reformulations performance was lower than with official sub-topics, but improvements over the initial ranking remained in most settings. [xQuAD](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2010/proceedings/www/p881.pdf)
- **[CLASSIC] [MEASURED]** PM-2 (Dang and Croft, SIGIR 2012) treats result slots as seats in an election. For each slot it picks the aspect with the largest Sainte-Laguë quotient v_i / (2 s_i + 1), where v_i is the aspect's weight and s_i the seats it already holds, then picks the document that best serves that aspect while also serving others, and credits each aspect with a share of the seat. Alpha-NDCG, Web track 2009 with official sub-topics: query likelihood 0.2979, MMR 0.2963, xQuAD 0.3300, PM-2 0.3473. Web track 2010: 0.3236, 0.3349, 0.4074, 0.4546; with aspects from query suggestions xQuAD 0.3582 and PM-2 0.4374. Sub-topic recall in 2010: 0.5479 for the baseline, 0.6410 for xQuAD, 0.6503 for PM-2. Wins and losses against the baseline for PM-2 were 34/10 in 2010 and 19/19 in 2009. The authors call MMR "the least effective". [PM-2](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1050)
- **[CLASSIC] [SNIPPET]** Diversification over time exists as an idea: Berberich and Bedathur (workshop paper, 2013) rank for relevance and for spread over publication times of interest; Gupta and Berberich (ECIR 2016) derive the time intervals of interest from temporal expressions in pseudo-relevant documents. No numbers were retrieved. A term-level follow-up by Dang and Croft reports that xQuAD and PM-2 can diversify with terms as aspects. [temporal diversification](https://typeset.io/papers/temporal-diversification-of-search-results-15vtj2wvl4); [Gupta and Berberich 2016](https://dhruvg.folk.ntnu.no/pub/dhruv-ecir-2016.pdf); [term-level review](https://liner.com/review/term-level-search-result-diversification)

**Diversity in retrieval-augmented generation**
- **[MEASURED] [EMB]** Wang et al. (Salesforce; arXiv v2 7 Apr 2025): MMR or farthest-point sampling before the reader. Recall of the answer-bearing sentence at a 5% keep rate: Natural Questions 46.28 to 50.43, TriviaQA 63.44 to 65.29, NarrativeQA 18.60 to 20.88. Chunk selection with E5 at the largest budget: NarrativeQA 13.42 to 22.56, TriviaQA 84.62 to 88.99, Natural Questions 70.7 to 71.5. Original document order was best for selected sentences. One setting went the other way (68.7 against 70.4 on SQuAD sentences). No multi-hop sets. [Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09017v2)
- **[MEASURED] [EMB, LLM-Q]** DF-RAG (Khan et al., arXiv 23 Jan 2026): an MMR variant whose relevance weight is chosen per query by an LLM planner and evaluator. F1 at about 500 words with Llama 3.3 70B, plain retrieval, fixed weight, per-query weight, oracle weight: HotpotQA 51.1, 53.3, 58.1, 69.5; MuSiQue 29.9, 30.9, 33.3, 46.2; 2Wiki 49.8, 54.8, 58.1, 65.3. The oracle weight is worth up to 18 F1 over plain retrieval; the best fixed weight differs by dataset; gains are smallest on MuSiQue, where later hops depend on earlier answers. 200 queries per set, single runs, no evidence recall reported. [DF-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17212)
- **[SNIPPET]** A determinantal-point-process selector reports +31.8% on 4-hop MultiHop-RAG queries (secondary summary only), and a 30-question practitioner test found full-evidence coverage moving from 80% to 85% with MMR after a cross-encoder, with 19 of 20 test questions unchanged. Typed-RAG (Mar 2025) and RichRAG (Jun 2024) obtain explicit aspects by having an LLM split or predict sub-aspects. [ScalDPP summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2604.03240); [practitioner post](https://blog.gopenai.com/i-added-mmr-after-cross-encoder-reranking-it-fixed-one-multi-hop-rag-case-not-the-original-dd8324d59a1b); [Typed-RAG](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15879); [RichRAG](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12566)

**Conversational memory: coverage evidence**
- **[MEASURED]** SmartSearch tried MMR reranking and reports that it "did not help" (no figure), and that pruning by cumulative score cost 2.3 points. Its budget curves: gold-evidence recall at 1K/2K/3K/4K words is .927/.958/.972/.979 on LoCoMo and .850/.928/.959/.973 on LongMemEval-S. In its oracle analysis retrieval recall was 98.6% but only 22.5% of gold evidence survived truncation to the token budget, which the authors call the compilation bottleneck and answer with neural rerankers. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1)
- **[MEASURED]** Threader is the one source found that reports complete-evidence coverage beside mean recall ("Full-R denotes the fraction of queries with complete evidence coverage, and Mean-R denotes average evidence recall"). Full pipeline, top 5 units: 82.55 against 90.26; top 12: 93.40 against 96.50. With BM25 as the only signal (dense and reranker removed): 73.19 against 81.83 at top 5 and 84.68 against 91.06 at top 12. Removing only BM25 from the full pipeline: 80.43 and 88.21 at top 5. The table does not name its benchmark; its accuracies match the paper's LongMemEval results. [Threader](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33226)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL]** EdgeMem mixes the global ranking with representatives from at least 3 distinct sessions, 5 for enumerative questions. This is a fixed coverage floor over sessions; it is part of the +8.44 step and is not isolated. [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM-W]** AutoViewMem uses a determinantal point process to pick views, not passages: queries with no gold evidence in the pool rose from 5.14% to 7.03% when views were chosen at random. [AutoViewMem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21940)
- **[PROPOSED]** xMemory (Hu et al., arXiv 2 Feb 2026) argues that agent memory is a bounded, coherent stream in which many spans are highly correlated or near duplicates, so flat top-k returns redundant context; it selects a compact backbone of complementary groups and expands to raw messages only when needed. The abstract gives no numbers. [xMemory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02007)
- **[MEASURED]** Eywa's LongMemEval-S error analysis attributes 12 of 59 failures to candidates lost before the final context and 7 to assembly across several memories, so about a third of its residual errors are packing or multi-evidence errors rather than retrieval misses. [Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771)

### Inferences
- The classic lesson transfers directly: do not rely on pairwise novelty; name the aspects and allocate slots to them. In the xQuAD study MMR fell below undiversified BM25.
- Aspects can be defined deterministically for a conversation: (a) query terms and phrases; (b) time buckets or sessions that contain matches, which turns PM-2 into proportional time-spread sampling; (c) the distinct values recorded for the queried key in the value lane, so every state of a plan gets a slot; (d) rare terms that recur in the first-pass results.
- Option (d) uses feedback terms as aspects for budget allocation among already-retrieved candidates instead of as new query terms. That keeps the coverage benefit of feedback without the drift measured in the first section, and no source was found that tests it.
- The right outcome measure is complete-evidence coverage at a fixed token budget. Threader's BM25-only gap of 8.6 points between mean recall and complete coverage at top 5 is the clearest published statement of headroom for a lexical system.
- SmartSearch's null result for MMR was obtained with a cross-encoder and budgets where recall is already .93 to .96. Diversification should matter most where the budget is tight and there is no neural ranker, which is the target system's regime; that is a hypothesis.
- DF-RAG's oracle result says the best relevance-diversity balance is query dependent. A deterministic proxy for the query type (enumerative or change-tracking cue words, as in EdgeMem's trigger) is the model-free counterpart.

### Gaps
- No application of xQuAD or PM-2 to retrieval-augmented generation or to conversational memory was found.
- No measurement of time-spread or session-proportional sampling in isolation was found.
- Most memory papers report recall-any or mean recall; complete-evidence coverage was found in one paper only.
- SmartSearch gives no numbers for its MMR test; DF-RAG gives no evidence recall; the determinantal-point-process figure is from a secondary summary.
- Budgeted submodular and knapsack selection is covered in the sibling note on selection and retrieval and was not re-researched here.

## Ideas from other fields worth transferring: information foraging theory, compression and minimum description length, sketching (count-min, minhash, bloom filters) for cheap "is this topic anywhere" tests, version control (blame, bisect) for tracing how a value changed, event sourcing and snapshots

### Takeaway
Each of these ideas has at most one LLM-era data point and none has been measured inside a model-free verbatim conversational memory: foraging appears as a trained search policy, description length only as software heuristics, sketches as an existence filter in one library, version control as commit, branch and rollback for agents without any blame or bisect analogue, and event sourcing as architecture papers without retrieval metrics.

### Cited Findings

**Information foraging**
- **[CLASSIC] [secondary source]** Pirolli and Card's patch model (1999) borrows the marginal value theorem: a forager should leave an information patch when the rate of gain from staying drops to the average rate available elsewhere, with the richness of the patch and the cost of switching setting the stopping point. Information scent is the perceived value of cues that guide where to go next. [review in "Information Farming", 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12544)
- **[MEASURED] [fully model-based]** InForage (Qian and Liu, NeurIPS 2025; arXiv May 2025) trains a search policy with reward R = efficiency x (outcome + 0.2 x information gain), where efficiency is 0.95 raised to the number of steps beyond two. Exact match with a 3B model: HotpotQA 40.9, 2Wiki 42.8, MuSiQue 17.2, eight-set average 41.0, against 35.6 for the strongest trained baseline and 26.5 for plain retrieval. Ablation values are shown only in a figure. Nothing in it is model-free. [InForage](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09316v2)

**Compression and description length**
- **[SNIPPET] [PROPOSED]** No paper applying minimum description length to context selection was found. A Rust crate documents a stage selector that minimises model cost plus data-given-model cost, citing Rissanen (1978); CompSelect (Feb 2025) frames clue selection by Occam's razor; another preprint cuts the context at natural gaps in the score distribution. [sqz-engine source](https://docs.rs/crate/sqz-engine/0.7.0/source/src/mdl_selector.rs); [CompSelect](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11811v7); [gap-based cutoff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09222v1)
- **[MEASURED] [NO MODEL]** Compression as a similarity measure: Jiang et al. (Findings of ACL 2023) classify text with gzip and nearest neighbours under the normalised compression distance (C(xy) - min(C(x), C(y))) / max(C(x), C(y)); competitive with non-pretrained neural models on six of seven in-distribution sets and ahead of BERT on several low-resource languages; limited by gzip's sliding window. Classification, not retrieval. [Jiang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09410)
- **[MEASURED] [model-based]** Learned compression used as a denoiser helped conversational retrieval in SeCom: removing it lowered GPT4Score from 69.33 to 59.87 on LoCoMo. [SeCom](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05589v1)

**Sketching**
- **[CLASSIC]** The count-min sketch (Cormode and Muthukrishnan, Journal of Algorithms 2005) answers point, range and inner-product queries in sublinear space and improved space bounds from order 1/epsilon squared to 1/epsilon. [count-min sketch](https://dimacs.rutgers.edu/~graham/pubs/html/CormodeMuthukrishnan04CMJalg.html)
- **[PROPOSED] [VENDOR]** Popoto, an agent-memory library, ships an existence filter (a Bloom filter, default capacity 100,000 and target error 0.01) that answers "do I know anything about X?" before an expensive query, with a batch check for many keywords in one round trip, and a frequency sketch (count-min, width 2000, depth 7). No measured numbers. [Popoto docs](https://popoto.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/existence-filter/)
- **[MEASURED]** LSHBloom (Khan et al., arXiv Nov 2024, v4 Jan 2026) replaces the MinHash locality-sensitive index with Bloom filters for near-duplicate detection: 12x faster and 18x less disk on the peS2o corpus at the same deduplication quality. [LSHBloom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04257)
- **[VENDOR]** Log search is the nearest production analogue of "is this token anywhere in this block". Grafana Loki added Bloom filters for needle-in-haystack queries, moved them in version 3.3 (21 Nov 2024) from n-grams of log text to structured-metadata key-value pairs because that is far smaller, reports filters under 1% of the raw structured metadata, and now labels the feature deprecated in its forward-looking docs. [Loki 3.3 blog](https://grafana.com/blog/2024/11/21/grafana-loki-3.3-release-faster-query-results-via-blooms-for-structured-metadata/); [Loki docs](https://grafana.com/docs/loki/next/operations/query-acceleration-blooms/)

**Version control**
- **[CLASSIC]** git bisect finds the commit where a property changed by binary search in about log2(N) tests (the manual's example: 675 revisions left, roughly 10 steps) and accepts custom terms such as old and new for any property. git log -L traces "the evolution of the line range" or of a function through history, and the pickaxe option finds commits that change the number of occurrences of a string. [git bisect](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-bisect); [git log](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-log)
- **[MEASURED] [LLM]** Git Context Controller (Wu et al., arXiv Jul 2025, v3 24 Jul 2026) gives agents COMMIT, BRANCH, MERGE and CONTEXT operations; the abstract reports over 13% better task resolution than long-context baselines and over 80% on SWE-Bench Verified. [GCC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00031)
- **[MEASURED, no numbers in abstract] [LLM]** ChronoMem (Su et al., arXiv 30 Jul 2026) commits a whole-memory snapshot at each write and maps natural-language undo requests to versions by hybrid lexical and semantic retrieval; it introduces a test of whether answers behave as if later updates never happened. [ChronoMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27773)
- **[PROPOSED] [VENDOR]** DiffMem keeps current memory as files and history in git, pulling diffs on demand; a Dolt essay (22 Jan 2026) argues for branch and merge over a graph of context; tools such as selvedge annotate code lines with the agent step and reasoning that introduced them. A targeted search found no system that applies a blame or bisect analogue to facts changing within a conversation. [DiffMem mirror](https://cnb.cool/8888/github.com/Growth-Kinetics/DiffMem); [Dolt](https://dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-22-agentic-memory); [selvedge](https://pypi.org/project/selvedge)

**Event sourcing and snapshots**
- **[CLASSIC]** Fowler (12 Dec 2005): record every state change as an ordered event; the log allows a complete rebuild, temporal queries that reconstruct past states, and replay to correct mistakes; snapshots let a system start from a recent point instead of replaying everything. [Fowler 2005](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)
- **[PROPOSED]** "The Log is the Agent" (Nakajima, arXiv 21 May 2026): the append-only log is the source of truth and the working graph a deterministic projection; replay, forking at any event without re-running the prefix, and lineage from goal to model call; no quantitative results. ESAA, DMF and Eywa follow the same pattern and are described in the views section. [Nakajima 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21997)
- **[MEASURED]** The only measured consequence found is consistency, not retrieval: Stashbird's 20 of 20 correct final states under updates against 8 of 20. [Stashbird](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34242v2)

### Inferences
- Foraging gives a stopping rule for neighbour expansion that fixed windows lack. Treat a session as a patch and keep reading adjacent spans while the lexical gain per token (new query terms, new aspects, new values) stays above the average gain of jumping to the next best seed. EdgeMem reads exactly one neighbour each side, and SmartSearch sizes its whole pack with one global threshold (0.03 times the top reranker score); an adaptive, deterministic, per-patch rule is untested.
- A compression distance is a model-free novelty estimate: the extra compressed bytes a span adds to the already selected set. It could serve as the redundancy term in greedy packing where MMR would use embedding similarity. No retrieval measurement exists, and short spans are a known weak spot of compressors.
- At single-conversation scale an exact inverted index already answers "is this topic anywhere" at no extra cost, so sketches earn their place only across many conversations (open-memex shows recall@5 falling from 97.0% to 40.6% when all haystacks are pooled, which makes routing to the right conversation the problem), or for collapsing near-duplicate tool output by MinHash before packing. Loki's retreat from text n-gram filters to key-value filters suggests filters over typed values are the economical form.
- The version-control mapping is direct for the value lane. Blame is a pointer from the current value of a key to the span that last set it. The line-history command is the ordered chain of spans where the value changed, which is the literal answer to "how did the plan change". Bisect is only worth having when testing a point in history is expensive; with a sorted value ledger the change points are read off directly.
- The target system is already event sourced. The transferable part is the snapshot: a periodic materialised state of the value ledger so that "as of turn t" questions do not require a scan.

### Gaps
- No model-free use of foraging theory for retrieval or packing was found.
- No measured use of description length or compression distance for context selection was found.
- No measurement of sketch-based existence tests in agent memory was found; Popoto's page gives configuration, not results.
- No blame or bisect analogue for conversational facts was found, in papers or tools.
- Event-sourcing papers for agents report reproducibility and consistency, not retrieval quality.
- Pirolli and Card's paper was not read directly; the patch-model description is from a 2026 review.

### Takeaway
The combination "verbatim turns, model-free construction, deterministic retrieval" is not novel as of late 2026 (EdgeMem, Zero-Mem, SmartSearch's substring path, DMF), so novelty has to come from a specific mechanism with an isolated measurement. The least occupied ideas with a plausible effect on multi-evidence questions are explicit-aspect slot allocation, a deterministic per-key change ledger, cross-span cover density, lexically seeded graph propagation without embeddings, and a foraging-style stopping rule.

**Evaluation facts that bear on a measurable claim**
- **[MEASURED]** LoCoMo leaves little room for multi-evidence mechanisms, and what room there is depends on budget and ranker: 97.0% of its oracle search traces are single-hop and 73.4% of questions cite one evidence entry. With neural rerankers and about 3,100 context tokens, entity hops and feedback each moved it by 0.3 points or less, and typed path search was null in PRISM. With no reranker and under 1,000 evidence tokens, EdgeMem's cross-session co-occurrence channel still added +2.21 [0.76, 3.50] strict-judge points. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1); [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12260v1); [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED]** LongMemEval-S separates systems on the relevant categories: multi-session 84.2 (SmartSearch index-free) against 47.4 (Zep) and 51.1 (full context); temporal reasoning 82.7 against 54.1 and 60.2. [SmartSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15599v1)
- **[MEASURED]** Set-level categories stay hard even with LLM extraction: on BEAM (700 rubric-scored questions over 35 conversations) Eywa's lowest categories were summarization 64.07%, knowledge update 70.00% and event ordering 79.49%, against 93.21% for contradiction resolution. [Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771)
- **[MEASURED]** Judge choice moves absolute scores a great deal: EdgeMem scores 61.01 under a strict judge and 77.86 under a lenient one on the same outputs, and its evidence-only reader abstains on 28.0% of answerable LoCoMo questions while refusing 92.57% of unsupported-premise questions (best baseline 84.68% under the same instruction). [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553)
- **[MEASURED]** Statistical practice worth copying: paired bootstrap intervals with wins, ties and losses per query (SPRIG), paired conversation-cluster bootstrap with 10 clusters (EdgeMem), and a robustness index for any expansion lane (Collins-Thompson). [SPRIG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23372); [EdgeMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05553); [Collins-Thompson 2009](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~kevynct/pubs/ir0537-collinsthompson.pdf)

### Inferences
- Five mechanisms are open and measurable in the target system, in rough order of expected value for "what did we try for X" and "how did the plan change":
  1. Explicit-aspect slot allocation in the pack. PM-2 seats over deterministic aspects (sessions with matches, distinct values of the queried key, recurring rare terms from the first pass). Report complete-evidence coverage and mean recall at a fixed token budget against score order, a fixed session floor and MMR. Prior art stops at EdgeMem's fixed floor.
  2. A per-key change ledger maintained incrementally from the value lane: ordered (turn, value, span id) with derived validity intervals, queried as the chain of change points plus neighbours. Test on LongMemEval knowledge-update and temporal questions and on BEAM event ordering and knowledge update. Prior art is LLM-derived (Zep, Stashbird), unablated (DMF) or unmeasured (Bitacorista).
  3. Cover density across adjacent spans as a candidate generator, compared with fixed neighbour reading at equal tokens. No prior art found.
  4. Propagation from BM25 seeds over a span to rare-key graph with a document-frequency band and no embeddings. Prior art uses embeddings in the propagation (Zero-Mem, LinearRAG) or a single lookup (EdgeMem).
  5. A marginal-gain stopping rule for neighbour expansion. No prior art found.
- A gated feedback lane is a sixth, lower-novelty option: apply expansion only when a first-pass confidence signal such as the top-score gap is weak, and report the robustness index. The gate itself is the untested part on conversational data.
- The comparison baseline that reviewers will expect is EdgeMem's ladder: flat span BM25, then neighbours and session mixing, then the new mechanism, with insertion-order checks because structure components overlap.
- A lexical-only system cannot borrow SmartSearch's answer to the packing problem (a cross-encoder moving the first gold passage from rank 195 to 8). Its equivalent must be better lexical ranking signals and coverage-aware packing, which is where mechanisms 1 to 3 act.
- Published model-free results use spaCy at query time for query keys. A regex for capitalised spans gave R@10 0.812 against 0.810 for spaCy in SPRIG's hybrid setting (HotpotQA, the bucket where an extracted entity matches a gold title; the buckets are not identical sets), so the dependency is probably removable; that should be confirmed on conversational text, where names are often lower-case or pronominal (a limitation SmartSearch states for its own named-entity dependence).

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found that targets "what did we try for X" or "how did the plan change" over real work logs such as coding sessions; LongMemEval multi-session and BEAM's event-ordering and summarization categories are the nearest proxies.
- No source reports complete-evidence coverage for a purely lexical pipeline at paragraph-span granularity; Threader's BM25-only row is at segment granularity and from a single paper.
- EdgeMem's LongMemEval baselines are transcribed from another paper and include no BM25 row, so the size of its advantage over plain BM25 on that benchmark is unknown.
- Several 2026 sources are single-group preprints without independent replication (EdgeMem, Zero-Mem, SmartSearch, DMF, Threader, Eywa, Stashbird); SPRIG has internal inconsistencies; AgentIR, Palimpzest and a few others were read only through summaries, as tagged.
- Searches were run in English only and on one day (2026-11-18); absence claims ("not found") mean no result surfaced in targeted searches, not proof of absence.
