# Map-reduce decomposition, task graphs and shared memory for LLM agents (2023 to 2026)

Notes on reading: every number below is tagged with its benchmark, baseline and model generation where the source gave them. "Abstract only" means I read only the arXiv abstract page and could not confirm details from the full text. All results are the papers' own measurements unless stated; almost none has independent replication.

## 1. Map-reduce style systems: unit, schema, combine rule, accuracy against a single long-context call

### Takeaway
The designs that beat a single long-context call share three features: each unit returns a fixed-shape record (not free text) that carries evidence and a comparable confidence or validity signal, the combine step is either deterministic code or a constrained fold over those records, and the original text stays addressable so details can be re-read. Purely parallel "summarise each chunk and merge" was measured as worse than sequential hand-off (Chain of Agents) unless the per-chunk record is structured (LLM×MapReduce), and the largest reported gains appear at lengths where the base model's own window fails or degrades.

### Cited Findings

**LLM×MapReduce (Tsinghua THUNLP, Oct 2024; Llama3-70B-Instruct and Qwen2-72B-Instruct)**
- Unit: one chunk of the long text. Each map call returns four fields: Extracted Information (query-relevant facts), Rationale (reasoning from the facts to the answer), Answer (or the literal "NO INFORMATION", which later stages drop), and a Confidence Score out of 5. This is the "structured information protocol", designed for inter-chunk dependency. — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- In-context confidence calibration: no parameters change; the prompt supplies scoring principles and worked examples so that claims directly supported by the text score high, model-inferred claims medium, unsupported claims low. The stated aim is one consistent scale across chunks so conflicting answers can be compared. — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- Combine rule: an optional "collapse" stage groups the N mapped records into K groups that fit the window and compresses each into a record of the same four-field shape, repeating until everything fits; the reduce call then answers from the collapsed records, using the calibrated confidence to settle conflicts. The paper gives no more precise conflict rule than "use the confidence scores". — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- Measured (InfiniteBench average, excluding Code.Run and Math.Calc): Llama3-70B × MapReduce 68.66, Qwen2-72B × MapReduce 65.97, GPT-4 57.34, Qwen2-72B direct 54.74, Kimi-Chat 51.89, Claude 2 51.62, Llama3-70B + LongAgent 53.81, Llama3-70B + Chain-of-Agents 15.97 (the last two are this paper's own runs of the competing methods). — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- Ablation (Llama3-70B): full method En.Avg 41.23, Code.Debug 62.94, Math.Find 91.43; without confidence calibration 39.18 / 58.12 / 90.00; without calibration and without the structured protocol 25.93 / 46.45 / 56.00. The structured record contributes far more than the calibration. — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- An 8K-trained Llama3-70B handled needle-in-a-haystack up to 1,280K tokens with the framework (figure only; per-length numbers not in the text). — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)

**Chain of Agents (Google, June 2024; PaLM 2 text-bison/unicorn, Gemini 1.0 Ultra, Claude 3 Haiku/Sonnet/Opus)**
- Unit: a chunk smaller than the worker's window. Workers run sequentially; each receives the previous worker's "communication unit" (CU), its own chunk and the query, and emits a new CU. The CU holds evidence for QA, a running summary for summarisation, and a code summary with function and class names for code completion. A manager sees only the last CU plus the query. — [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)
- Measured: text-unicorn MuSiQue F1 CoA 42.49 vs RAG 40.38 vs truncated "Vanilla" 29.67; Qasper F1 38.01 vs 24.44 vs 27.81; Gemini Ultra QuALITY accuracy 80.60 vs 62.40 vs 57.40 (8k); NarrativeQA F1 25.26 vs 14.51 vs 7.71. Against a true long-context call: Claude 3 Haiku NarrativeQA CoA 18.80 vs 7.17 with the full 200k window. — [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)
- Combine-rule ablations: removing the manager drops MuSiQue 37.09 to 26.79; feeding the manager all CUs, or letting the last worker answer, was worse; right-to-left or random chunk order was worse than left-to-right; parallel variants ("Merge", "Hierarchical") underperformed because workers cannot communicate. — [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)
- Cost model: full-context encoding O(n²) vs CoA O(nk) for input length n and window k. — [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)

**LongAgent (Fudan, Feb 2024; fine-tuned LLaMA2-7B, also GPT-3.5 by prompting)**
- Unit: fixed-size chunk per member (4k window), dozens to hundreds of members sharing one model. A leader decomposes the query and each round picks one of three actions: NEW_STATE (ask for more), CONFLICT, or ANSWER. — [arXiv 2402.11550 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11550v2)
- Conflict rule: the leader identifies members whose answers disagree; those members exchange chunks pairwise and answer again. Members that hallucinated mostly corrected themselves after seeing the relevant chunk. Inter-member communication raised average accuracy by 18.9% over 1k to 128k inputs. — [arXiv 2402.11550 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11550v2)
- Measured (Needle-in-a-Haystack PLUS, 1k to 128k, vs GPT-4 with full context): single-document 81.53% vs 62.00%; multi-document 55.33% vs 50.37%. Training members with "refuse" examples raised correct refusals from 51.0% to 78.6%. Chunk sizes of 500 to about 2,000 tokens reduced hallucination; larger chunks increased it. — [arXiv 2402.11550 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11550v2)

**ReadAgent (Google DeepMind, Feb 2024)**
- Three steps: the model chooses episode boundaries (pagination), compresses each episode to a "gist memory", and can look up original pages when detail is needed. Outperforms retrieval, original long context and gist-only baselines on QuALITY, NarrativeQA and QMSum, extending effective context 3.5 to 20×. (Abstract only; no per-benchmark numbers confirmed.) — [arXiv 2402.09727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09727)

**LCM, Lossless Context Management (Voltropy, paper dated 14 Feb 2026; Claude Opus 4.6 with Haiku 4.5 as auxiliary)**
- Data structures: an Immutable Store holding every message and tool result verbatim; an Active Context of recent raw messages plus summary nodes; a summary DAG with leaf summaries (over message spans) and condensed summaries (over summaries), each with provenance links and stable IDs that are inserted into the active context so `lcm_expand` can recover the originals. Stored in embedded PostgreSQL with transactional writes and full-text search. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- Summaries escalate through three levels until output is smaller than input: detail-preserving LLM summary, bullet-point summary at half the target, then deterministic truncation to 512 tokens. Soft threshold triggers asynchronous compaction, hard threshold blocks. Files over about 25k tokens stay out of context and are represented by an ID, path and a type-aware "exploration summary". — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- `llm_map(input_path, prompt, output_schema, …)`: input is a JSONL file; each item is one stateless, tool-free model call; default concurrency 16; each output is validated against a JSON Schema and, on failure, the validation error is fed back and the item retried up to a limit; items are tracked pending/running/completed/failed under pessimistic locking; results go to an output JSONL registered in the store, and the calling agent receives only a summary handle. `agentic_map` is the same pipeline with a full tool-using sub-agent per item and a `read_only` flag. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- Combine rule: none in the model. `llm_reduce` and `agentic_reduce` were deliberately not built; the authors recommend deterministic code for reduction. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- Delegation guard: a non-root agent that spawns a sub-agent must declare `delegated_scope` and `kept_work`; if it cannot name work it keeps, the call is rejected. Only the sub-agent's final answer returns to the parent. `lcm_expand` is restricted to sub-agents so the main loop is not flooded. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- Measured (OOLONG, trec_coarse split, Volt vs Claude Code v2.1.4, both on Opus 4.6): average absolute score 74.8 vs 70.3; gain over raw Opus +29.2 vs +24.7. By length (gain over raw Opus): 8K +11.2 vs +13.1 and 16K +25.0 vs +26.3 (Claude Code slightly ahead); 256K +18.5 vs +8.5; 512K +42.4 vs +29.8; 1M +51.3 vs +47.0. Raw Opus 4.6 degrades past about 65K tokens and falls below 20 at the longest lengths. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)
- Stated limitations: Opus 4.6 sometimes recognised OOLONG data and answered from memory, so contaminated tasks were excluded and the raw results show a narrower gap; one benchmark split only; "lossless" means retrievable, with no guarantee the agent will retrieve. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)

**Recursive Language Models (MIT, Zhang, Kraska, Khattab; v1 31 Dec 2025, v3 May 2026; GPT-5, Qwen3-Coder-480B)**
- The prompt is loaded as a variable in a Python REPL; the root model sees only metadata such as length, writes code to inspect and split it, calls sub-models via `llm_query`, and returns with `FINAL()` or `FINAL_VAR()`. The unit and combine rule are whatever code the model writes. Recursion depth is one and sub-calls are synchronous in the evaluated version. — [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1)
- Measured (GPT-5; base model / CodeAct+BM25 / summary agent / RLM / RLM without sub-calls): CodeQA 24.00 / 22.00 / 58.00 / 62.00 / 58.00; BrowseComp+ (1K docs) 0.00 / 51.00 / 70.47 / 91.33 / 88.00; OOLONG 44.00 / 38.00 / 46.00 / 56.50 / 36.00; OOLONG-Pairs 0.04 / 24.67 / 0.01 / 58.00 / 43.93. — [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1)
- Cost: median RLM run cheaper than the base call for GPT-5 but with a long tail; up to 3× cheaper than the summary baseline. RLM was slightly worse than the base model on short inputs. — [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1)
- Later abstract (v3): GPT-5 with RLM is a median 26% above compaction, 130% above CodeAct with sub-calls and 13% above Claude Code across four tasks; a post-trained RLM-Qwen3-8B beats base Qwen3-8B by 28.3% on average. — [arXiv 2512.24601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601)
- LCM's framing of the difference: RLM lets the model write its own loops (flexible, higher variance, always-on overhead); LCM gives engine-run operators (termination guarantees, no overhead on short tasks). — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)

**DocETL (UC Berkeley, Oct 2024, v3 Apr 2025; gpt-4o-mini pipelines)**
- Operators: Map (adds output keys to each document's schema), Parallel Map, Reduce (groups by key and folds in batches with a scratchpad carrying state; an `associative: False` flag preserves order), Filter, Resolve (a comparison prompt finds variant entity names, a resolution prompt picks the canonical one, before reduce), Equijoin, Unnest, Split (adds chunk ID and number), and Gather (attaches neighbouring chunks or summaries to each chunk as peripheral context). — [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- Validation: users attach Python checks to operators and failed outputs are retried with feedback; "gleaning" has a validator model critique an output and the operator refine it for up to k rounds. — [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- The optimiser rewrites pipelines with directives (split-map-gather-reduce, multi-level aggregation, duplicate-key resolution before reduce, isolating independent subtasks into parallel maps), rates candidate plans 1 to 5 and compares the top 6 pairwise. — [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- Measured: CUAD legal contracts (50 contracts) F1 0.477 optimised vs 0.393 for LOTUS; recall up 67% over the unoptimised pipeline; the optimised plan was 21 isolated maps plus a reduce. BioDEX rank precision up 33 to 80%. Optimised pipelines cost more and run slower; optimisation itself cost $1.58. — [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3). Abstract claim: plans 25 to 80% more accurate than well-engineered baselines across four tasks. — [arXiv 2410.12189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12189)

**LOTUS semantic operators (Stanford/Berkeley, July 2024, v3 Mar 2025; Llama-3-70B oracle, Llama-8B and TinyLlama proxies)**
- Operators, each parameterised by a natural-language expression: `sem_filter`, `sem_join`, `sem_topk` (pairwise comparisons with quick-select), `sem_agg` (requires a commutative, associative function; reference algorithm is a hierarchical reduce), `sem_group_by`, `sem_map`/`sem_extract`. — [arXiv 2407.11418 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11418v3)
- Accuracy contract: cheap proxies (small model or embeddings) handle most rows with fallback to the expensive model; the user sets recall and precision targets and a failure probability δ (defaults 0.9 and 0.2); thresholds are learned by sampling, and observed failure rates stayed under δ. — [arXiv 2407.11418 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11418v3)
- Measured: FEVER fact-checking 91.0% vs FacTool 80.9%, 190 s batched vs 5,396 s; SciFact nDCG@10 0.765 vs 0.741 for a re-ranker; BioDEX RP@5 0.265 vs DocETL 0.262, with DocETL failing about 1 in 3 runs in this comparison; proxy-only group-by was 17.4× faster but about 39% less accurate. — [arXiv 2407.11418 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11418v3)

**Palimpzest (MIT, May 2024; GPT-4 baseline)**
- Declarative queries with an optimiser trading runtime, cost and quality. A prototype plan ran 3.3× faster and 2.9× cheaper than baseline with better quality; with parallelism, up to 90.3× faster at 9.1× lower cost than single-threaded GPT-4, with F1 within 83.5% of that baseline (Legal Discovery, Real Estate Search, Medical Schema Matching). (Abstract only.) — [arXiv 2405.14696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14696)

### Inferences
- The evidence favours a typed per-unit record over free-text notes: LLM×MapReduce's ablation attributes roughly 15 points of English QA and 35 points of Math.Find to the four-field record, and only 2 to 5 points to calibration. LCM's schema validation with retry is the engineering version of the same idea.
- Two families solve cross-chunk dependency differently: sequential hand-off of a running record (Chain of Agents, DocETL fold with scratchpad) keeps order and dependency but gives up parallelism; parallel map with a record rich enough to merge later (LLM×MapReduce, `llm_map`, `sem_agg`) keeps parallelism but requires the merge function to be associative or the record to carry its own evidence.
- The systems with the strongest long-length results all moved the combine step away from an unconstrained model call: deterministic code (LCM, RLM's REPL), associative hierarchical reduce (LOTUS), or a fold over fixed-shape records (LLM×MapReduce collapse).
- Gains against a single call are length-dependent. LCM shows Claude Code slightly ahead at 8K and 16K; RLM is slightly worse than the base model on short inputs. Decomposition is not free below the point where the window degrades.
- Chain of Agents scoring 15.97 inside the LLM×MapReduce paper, against its own strong reported results, shows how sensitive these comparisons are to who implements the baseline and with which model.

### Gaps
- ReadAgent and Palimpzest details are abstract-level only; I did not confirm ReadAgent's per-benchmark numbers or Palimpzest's schema and operator definitions.
- No paper found compares all of these on one benchmark with one model generation. OOLONG is shared by RLM (GPT-5) and LCM (Opus 4.6) but with different splits and baselines, so the numbers are not comparable.
- The LCM paper's arXiv identifier (2605.04050) implies a May 2026 listing while the paper is dated 14 Feb 2026; I could not verify the listing date. Its results are from the vendor of the system under test.
- LLM×MapReduce gives no algorithm for how confidence settles a conflict (for example highest score wins, or weighted), only that the reduce prompt sees the scores.

## 2. Task decomposition into dependency graphs executed in parallel

### Takeaway
Dependencies are almost always represented the same way: a list of tasks with IDs, where an argument that depends on an earlier task is a placeholder naming that task's ID, substituted by non-model code once the earlier task finishes. Measured speedups from running independent nodes together are in the 1.4× to 3.7× range, and accuracy tends to rise modestly because the planner is not distracted by intermediate observations; the cost is that a plan made up front can be wrong, and designs that assume units are independent lose quality on tasks needing step-by-step reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- **HuggingGPT (Microsoft, Mar 2023; gpt-3.5-turbo, text-davinci-003, GPT-4):** the planner emits a JSON list of tasks with four slots, `task`, `id`, `dep` (IDs of prerequisite tasks) and `args`; a dependent argument is written as the placeholder `<resource>-task_id` and replaced at execution; independent tasks run in parallel. Planning quality: single task F1 54.45 (GPT-3.5) vs 29.44 (Vicuna-7b) vs 4.88 (Alpaca-7b); sequential F1 51.92 vs 22.89 vs 22.80; graph-shaped F1 51.91 vs 18.66 vs 20.59. — [arXiv 2303.17580 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17580)
- **ReWOO (May 2023; GPT-3.5, LLaMA-7B):** separates the reasoning plan from tool observations; reported 5× token efficiency and 4% accuracy improvement on HotpotQA, and showed the planning ability can be distilled from 175B GPT-3.5 into a 7B LLaMA. (Abstract only.) — [arXiv 2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323)
- **Skeleton-of-Thought (Tsinghua/Microsoft, July 2023, ICLR 2024; 12 models):** the model writes a skeleton of points, then each point is expanded in a parallel call. Speedup above 2× on 8 of 12 models, maximum 2.39×. Quality at least as good as normal generation in about 60% of cases; good on generic, common-sense, knowledge, roleplay and counterfactual questions; poor on writing, Fermi, math and coding. Aggregated answers are 1 to 2× longer and prefill tokens rise. — [arXiv 2307.15337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15337v3)
- Skeleton-of-Thought explicitly assumes the points are independent and ignores dependencies between them; the authors name a graph structure as the fix, and add a router (GPT-4 prompt or a trained RoBERTa) that decides per question whether to decompose at all. — [arXiv 2307.15337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15337v3)
- **Graph of Thoughts (ETH, Aug 2023, AAAI 2024):** thoughts are vertices and dependencies are edges of an arbitrary graph, allowing thoughts to be merged and refined in loops; on sorting, quality 62% higher than Tree of Thoughts at more than 31% lower cost. (Abstract only.) — [arXiv 2308.09687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09687)
- **LLMCompiler (Berkeley, Dec 2023, ICML 2024; GPT-3.5-turbo-1106, GPT-4-turbo-1106, GPT-4-0613, LLaMA-2 70B):** a planner emits a DAG of tool calls in which a dependent task uses a placeholder such as `$1` for an earlier output; a Task Fetching Unit, with no model involved, dispatches each task as soon as its dependencies resolve and substitutes real values; an executor runs them concurrently; results can go back to the planner for replanning. The planner streams, so tasks start before the plan is complete. — [arXiv 2312.04511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04511v3)
- LLMCompiler measured (speedup vs ReAct, cost reduction vs ReAct): HotpotQA 62.00% accuracy, 1.80×, 3.37× cheaper; Movie Recommendation 77.13% (ReAct 68.60%), 3.74×, 6.73× cheaper; ParallelQA 89.38%, 2.15×, 4.65× cheaper; Game of 24 (GPT-4, with replanning, vs Tree of Thoughts) 75.33%, 2.89×. OpenAI's built-in parallel function calling gave 1.61×, 2.76× and 1.86× on the first three. Streaming cut ParallelQA latency from 21.72 s to 16.69 s. WebShop success 19.8% to 48.2% (GPT-3.5) and 35.2% to 55.6% (GPT-4). — [arXiv 2312.04511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04511v3)
- LLMCompiler failure breakdown on ParallelQA (36 failures, about 10.6%): executor 64% (mostly math-tool errors such as wrong attributes or unit conversions), final answer step 28%, planner 8%. — [arXiv 2312.04511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04511v3)
- **TDAG (Feb 2024, accepted in Neural Networks):** decomposes a task dynamically and generates a sub-agent per subtask; evaluated on ItineraryBench; claims to outperform baselines significantly. (Abstract only; no numbers confirmed.) — [arXiv 2402.10178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10178)
- **LCM (Feb 2026):** `Tasks(tasks[])` runs two or more independent sub-agents in parallel, and each returns only its final answer to the parent. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)

### Inferences
- The placeholder-by-ID convention (`<resource>-0`, `$1`) is the common dependency representation from March 2023 onward. It makes the edge explicit and lets plain code, not a model, do scheduling and value substitution; LLMCompiler's Task Fetching Unit is the clearest statement of that split.
- In LLMCompiler's failure breakdown the planner caused 8% of failures and the final combine step 28%. On that benchmark the graph was rarely wrong; the join was a more frequent source of error than the decomposition.
- Skeleton-of-Thought is the measured case for "independence assumed, quality lost": speed rises about 2× but math and coding answers get worse, and the paper's remedy is to route such questions away from decomposition.
- All speedups above were measured on 2023-generation models (GPT-3.5/4, LLaMA-2). I found no re-measurement on 2025 to 2026 models, where native parallel tool calling is standard and the baseline is therefore faster.

### Gaps
- ReWOO's evidence-variable syntax and Graph of Thoughts' operation set were not confirmed from full text. From my recollection (unverified here), ReWOO plans use `#E1`, `#E2` variables referenced by later steps, the same pattern as LLMCompiler's `$1`.
- "Plan-and-execute" as a named pattern (LangChain, Plan-and-Solve prompting) and "dynamic task graph" systems beyond TDAG were not researched; no sourced numbers.
- No source found that measures how often an up-front plan must be revised, or the accuracy cost of executing a stale plan, on current models.

## 3. Blackboards, shared memory and multi-agent memory: what is shared, who writes, staleness and conflict

### Takeaway
The 2025 blackboard revivals share a board of requests or messages and let agents self-select, but their conflict handling is thin: either the requesting agent simply chooses which responses to use, or a dedicated LLM agent is asked to spot contradictions and another to delete clutter. Memory stores handle conflict at write time by an LLM choosing add, update, delete or no-op against the nearest existing entries, usually overwriting without version history; true concurrent-writer control (versions, isolation, ordering) is identified in 2026 position papers as unsolved and unmeasured.

### Cited Findings

**Blackboards**
- **Blackboard for data discovery (UMass Amherst and Google Cloud AI Research, Sept 2025, v2 Jan 2026; Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Claude 4 Opus, Qwen3-Coder):** the main agent posts requests to a shared blackboard without naming a recipient; helper agents (each owning a cluster of data-lake files, plus a web-search agent) each judge independently whether they can contribute. Helpers write to a separate response board readable only by the main agent, which the paper says prevents cross-influence between helpers. — [arXiv 2510.01285 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01285v2)
- Combine rule in that system: responses return as the observation of the main agent's "request help" action and the main agent decides whether to use or ignore each; no further conflict mechanism is described, and requests have no formal schema. — [arXiv 2510.01285 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01285v2)
- Measured (macro-average success over KramaBench and modified DSBench and DA-Code; single agent / RAG / master-slave / blackboard): Claude 4 Opus 4.11 / 23.00 / 27.63 / 31.43%; Gemini 2.5 Pro 7.71 / 18.51 / 24.01 / 28.53%; Gemini 2.5 Flash 4.32 / 14.52 / 14.49 / 16.54%; Qwen3-Coder 1.21 / 3.87 / 5.03 / 7.90%. File-discovery F1 (Gemini 2.5 Pro) 0.561 vs 0.513 master-slave vs 0.247 RAG. Cost about 2.3× RAG and 1.8× master-slave per question at similar runtime (132 to 145 s). — [arXiv 2510.01285 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01285v2). Abstract summary: 13 to 57% relative improvement in end-to-end success and up to 9% relative gain in discovery F1 over the best baseline. — [arXiv 2510.01285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01285)
- **LbMAS, general problem solving on a blackboard (Han and Zhang, July 2025; Llama-3.1-70b and Qwen-2.5-72b):** a public space every agent may read and write, plus private spaces for agents in a debate or verification; an LLM control unit picks who acts each round from the query, the board contents and agent descriptions; fixed roles are planner, decider, critic, conflict-resolver and cleaner, plus generated experts; at most 4 rounds. — [arXiv 2507.01701 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01701v1)
- Conflict and staleness in LbMAS: the conflict-resolver names agents whose messages contradict and sends them to a private space to discuss; the cleaner deletes useless or redundant messages, and an ablation found that marking messages instead of removing them lowered performance. The final answer comes from the decider, or from all agents answering with the answer most similar to the others chosen. — [arXiv 2507.01701 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01701v1)
- LbMAS measured: average 81.68 over MMLU, ARC-Challenge, GPQA-Diamond, BBH, MATH and GSM8K vs Chateval 80.56 and chain-of-thought 80.09; on MATH, 4.72M tokens vs Chateval 5.45M, Exchange-of-Thought 8.33M, AFlow 16.7M. Full consensus was reached on 89.8% of MMLU, 52.5% of GPQA and 29.4% of MATH problems. — [arXiv 2507.01701 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01701v1)
- **MetaGPT (Aug 2023, ICLR 2024; GPT-4):** a global message pool with publish-subscribe; agents publish structured documents (requirements, designs, interface definitions) and each role subscribes to the message types relevant to it, so any agent can pull what it needs without one-to-one queries. — [arXiv 2308.00352 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.00352v6)

**Memory stores**
- **Mem0 (Apr 2025; GPT-4o-mini):** extraction takes the latest message pair, a rolling conversation summary and the last 10 messages, and produces candidate facts. For each fact the system retrieves the 10 most similar stored memories and the model chooses, via tool call, ADD (nothing equivalent exists), UPDATE (complementary information; replace only if the new fact is richer), DELETE (the new fact contradicts an existing memory, which is removed) or NOOP. — [arXiv 2504.19413 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- The graph variant marks contradicted relationships invalid instead of deleting them, which the paper says enables temporal reasoning. — [arXiv 2504.19413 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- Mem0 measured (LOCOMO, LLM-judge score): full context 72.90%, graph variant 68.44%, Mem0 66.88%, Zep 65.99%, best RAG 60.97%, LangMem 58.10%, OpenAI memory 52.90%. p95 latency 1.44 s vs 17.12 s for full context; tokens per query 1,764 vs 26,031. Memory accuracy is below the full-context call; the gain is latency and cost. — [arXiv 2504.19413 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- **A-MEM (Feb 2025, NeurIPS 2025; GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, small Qwen and Llama models):** each note is a tuple of content, timestamp, model-generated keywords, tags and contextual description, an embedding, and a set of links. New notes are linked by taking the 10 nearest notes by cosine similarity and having the model decide which links to create. "Memory evolution" lets the model rewrite a neighbour's context, keywords and tags; the evolved note replaces the original, and the paper does not say prior versions are kept. About 1,200 tokens per memory operation vs about 16,900 per question for the LoCoMo and MemGPT baselines. — [arXiv 2502.12110 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110v11)
- **MIRIX (July 2025):** six memory types (Core, Episodic, Semantic, Procedural, Resource, Knowledge Vault) with a multi-agent framework coordinating updates and retrieval; 85.4% on LOCOMO, and on ScreenshotVQA 35% higher accuracy than a RAG baseline with 99.9% less storage. (Abstract only.) — [arXiv 2507.07957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07957)
- **G-Memory (June 2025; Qwen-2.5-7b/14b, gpt-4o-mini; AutoGen, DyLAN, MacNet):** three linked graphs. Interaction graph: nodes are single utterances tagged with the speaking agent, edges link an utterance to those it prompted. Query graph: nodes are past tasks with status Resolved or Failed and a pointer to their interaction graph. Insight graph: nodes are distilled lessons with the set of queries that support them. — [arXiv 2506.07398 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07398v2)
- G-Memory retrieval goes both ways from similar past queries: up to insights, and down to trajectories that a model trims to their core; each agent then receives a role-filtered slice. After a task, the run is stored, a query node is added, and a model writes new insights linked to the insights used. Failed runs are kept and contrasted with similar successes. — [arXiv 2506.07398 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07398v2)
- G-Memory measured: up to +20.89 points on ALFWorld (MacNet, Qwen-2.5-14b, 58.21% to 79.10%) and up to +10.12 on HotpotQA; removing interaction-level memory cost 4.47 points and removing insights 3.95. Retrieving more than 1 to 2 similar queries added noise. Single-agent memories (Voyager, MemoryBank) lowered multi-agent performance on PDDL with AutoGen. Multi-agent trajectories can be about 10× longer in tokens than single-agent ones. — [arXiv 2506.07398 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07398v2)
- **Collaborative Memory (Accenture, May 2025; gpt-4o):** two tiers, private fragments visible only to the originating user and shared fragments. Each fragment is a key (short topic) and value (explanation) with immutable provenance: creation time, contributing user, contributing agents, resources accessed. Access is a pair of time-varying bipartite graphs (user to agent, agent to resource); a fragment is readable only if the reader currently has rights to everything in its provenance. Separate LLM write policies for each tier; the shared one strips user-specific detail. — [arXiv 2505.18279 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18279v1)
- Collaborative Memory measured: on MultiHop-RAG (5 users, 6 agents) accuracy stays above 0.90 while resource calls drop by up to 61% at 50% query overlap and 59% at 75%. The paper does not address conflicting fragments, and says settings with frequent concurrency were not explored. — [arXiv 2505.18279 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18279v1)

**Consistency and concurrency (position papers, no measurements)**
- A March 2026 UCSD position paper frames multi-agent memory as shared (one pool, needs coherence support) versus distributed (local memories with selective sync, risks divergence), and calls consistency the largest open problem, split into read-time conflict handling (stale versions remain visible) and update-time visibility and ordering of concurrent writes. It notes access rules (permissions, scope, read-only vs read-write, unit of access) are under-specified, and recommends making versioning, visibility and conflict rules explicit. — [arXiv 2603.10062 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10062v1)
- A June 2026 position paper argues that many multi-agent failures blamed on coordination are classical concurrency anomalies (stale reads, lost updates, inconsistent state), made worse by long inference windows between read and write, and that frameworks should add conflict detection, isolation guarantees and structured access to shared resources. No experiments. — [arXiv 2608.18092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18092)
- Practitioner guidance (2026 vendor blogs, opinion not measurement): most teams use last-write-wins and accept occasional stale reads; version-based optimistic locking is suggested for write conflicts, which are distinguished from semantic conflicts. — [GreenNode](https://greennode.ai/blog/how-to-design-shared-memory-for-multi-agent-ai); [Hyperspell](https://www.hyperspell.com/blog/multi-agent-memory)

### Inferences
- Three write disciplines appear. Single-writer-per-surface (the data-discovery blackboard: main agent writes requests, helpers write to a response board only the main agent reads; LCM: sub-agents return one final answer) avoids conflict by construction. Open boards (LbMAS, MetaGPT pool) need added roles to resolve contradictions and prune. Shared stores (Mem0, A-MEM) resolve at write time with a model call against nearest neighbours.
- Two staleness policies were measured in opposite directions for different purposes: LbMAS found deleting clutter beat marking it, for a short-lived working board; Mem0's graph variant marks relations invalid rather than deleting and scored slightly higher (68.44 vs 66.88) on a long-lived store with temporal questions. The sources support "delete from the working surface, invalidate in the durable record", though neither paper tests that combination.
- Provenance on every entry (Collaborative Memory's immutable contributor, resource and time attributes; LCM's summary-to-source links; G-Memory's insight-to-query support sets) is the recurring mechanism that lets a reader check or discount an entry later. Stores without it (A-MEM overwriting neighbours' descriptions) cannot audit a bad rewrite.
- Isolating helpers from each other's answers is a deliberate design choice in the strongest blackboard result, in tension with Chain of Agents' finding that workers that cannot communicate do worse. The difference is task shape: independent lookups versus a chain of dependent evidence.
- None of the memory papers measures concurrent writers. Mem0, A-MEM and MIRIX are evaluated as a single writer on conversation benchmarks; "used by several agents" is an architectural claim, not a tested one.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023 to 2026 paper implementing tuple-space or Linda-style coordination for LLM agents, and none measuring a shared knowledge graph written by several agents at once; I ran no dedicated search for either, so absence here is weak evidence.
- MIRIX's manager agents and which agent writes which store were not confirmed (abstract only). A-MEM's per-category LoCoMo figures could not be read reliably from the table.
- Two items surfaced only in search snippets and were not opened: a 2026 survey of persistent agent memory (arXiv 2606.30306) and a "CoAgent" paper (arXiv 2606.15376) said to argue that locks and optimistic concurrency fit agent workflows poorly because transactions span minutes of inference. Treat both as unverified leads.
- No measured comparison of shared versus private memory on task accuracy beyond Collaborative Memory's resource-call reduction.

## 4. Agent-to-agent artifact protocols and evidence on structured versus free-text hand-offs

### Takeaway
A2A and MCP standardise the envelope (task state, typed parts, artifacts with IDs, optional JSON output schema) but not the content: neither defines confidence, evidence or provenance fields, so a hand-off contract still has to be designed on top. Evidence that structure between units reduces errors is real but indirect (ablations inside single systems), and one 2026 small-model study found forcing JSON between agents made accuracy much worse, so the benefit depends on the model being able to fill the schema reliably and on validation at the boundary.

### Cited Findings

**A2A (specification version 1.0.0; earlier 0.3.0, 0.2.6, 0.1.0)**
- Task: `id` (server-generated), optional `contextId` grouping related tasks and messages, `status` (state, optional message, timestamp), optional `artifacts`, `history`, `metadata`. States: SUBMITTED, WORKING, COMPLETED, FAILED, CANCELED, INPUT_REQUIRED, REJECTED, AUTH_REQUIRED; the first four after WORKING are terminal, the last two of the list are interrupted states. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- Message: `messageId`, `role` (user or agent), `parts`, optional `contextId`, `taskId`, `referenceTaskIds`, `extensions`, `metadata`. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- Part: exactly one of `text`, `raw` (bytes), `url`, or `data` (arbitrary JSON value), plus optional `mediaType`, `filename`, `metadata`. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- Artifact: `artifactId` (unique within a task), optional `name` and `description`, `parts` (at least one), optional `metadata` and `extensions`. Streaming uses TaskArtifactUpdateEvent with `append` (add to a previously sent artifact of the same ID) and `lastChunk`; events must be delivered in generation order. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- AgentCard: name, description, version, supported interfaces, capabilities (streaming, push notifications), security schemes, default input and output media types, optional signatures, and a list of skills; each AgentSkill has id, name, description, tags, optional examples and per-skill input and output modes. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- Structured output in A2A is by convention: the client lists `acceptedOutputModes` (media types), and the spec's example has the client put `mediaType: application/json` and a JSON `schema` in a part's metadata, with the agent returning JSON serialised in a text part. No required schema validation for `data` parts was found in the portion read. — [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)

**MCP (specification revision 2025-06-18)**
- A tool declares `inputSchema` and an optional `outputSchema` (JSON Schema). Results may carry unstructured `content` (text, image, audio, resource links, embedded resources) and a `structuredContent` JSON object. If an output schema is given, servers MUST return conforming structured results and clients SHOULD validate them; for compatibility the JSON should also be serialised in a text block. Tool execution failures are reported in-band with `isError: true`. — [MCP specification, tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)
- The spec notes `structuredContent` is server-produced data and unrelated to schema-constrained model generation. — [MCP specification, tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)

**Evidence for and against structure between units**
- For: LLM×MapReduce ablation (Oct 2024, Llama3-70B): removing the structured per-chunk record (and calibration) cut InfiniteBench English QA from 41.23 to 25.93 and Math.Find from 91.43 to 56.00. — [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- For (claim, not isolated): MetaGPT states agents "communicate through documents and diagrams (structured outputs) rather than dialogue"; on SoftwareDev it scored executability 3.75 vs ChatDev 2.25 and human revision cost 0.83 vs 2.5, but the paper has no ablation isolating structured outputs from roles and executable feedback. — [arXiv 2308.00352 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.00352v6)
- For (engineering practice): LCM validates each per-item output against a JSON Schema and retries with the validation error; DocETL attaches code checks to operator outputs and retries with feedback. Neither reports an ablation without validation. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1); [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- Against: a July 2026 preprint on a five-role pipeline with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on GSM8K reports multi-agent accuracy falling from 75.0% to 45.0% with JSON inter-agent messages and recovering to 82.0% with plain text; two-call self-refinement reached 86.2% at 7.4× fewer tokens. (Abstract only; single 7B model, not peer reviewed.) — [arXiv 2607.26922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26922)
- Structured hand-off does not remove inter-agent failure: in the MAST taxonomy (1,600+ traces, 7 frameworks, 2025), inter-agent misalignment is 36.94% of failures, and the one structured protocol the authors describe (HyperAgent's two-field Context and Request message) appears inside a system that still fails. — [arXiv 2503.13657 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)

### Inferences
- The protocols give identity and lifecycle (artifact IDs, task states, append/last-chunk), which is what a combine step needs to know that a unit finished and which output is which. They do not give epistemic fields. A decomposition design must add its own evidence, confidence and source-pointer fields, as LLM×MapReduce and Collaborative Memory do.
- MCP is stricter than A2A on structure: a declared output schema is binding on the server, while A2A's JSON schema is a metadata convention.
- The conflicting results on JSON are consistent with one reading: structure helps when the model fills it reliably and a validator catches failures (large models, schema check and retry), and hurts when a small model's malformed or lossy JSON propagates unchecked through several stages.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on a 2025 to 2026 frontier model comparing free-text against schema-validated hand-offs with everything else fixed.
- The final part of the A2A specification (about the last fifth of the page) was not read; the release date of version 1.0.0 was not confirmed.
- "Structured handoff contracts" in agent frameworks (OpenAI Agents SDK handoffs, LangGraph state schemas, CrewAI task outputs) were not researched.

## 5. Known failure modes of decomposition

### Takeaway
The measured failure modes are: parallel units that cannot see each other's evidence (worse than a sequential chain), units that fabricate an answer when their chunk has none, units assumed independent when they are not, repeated work, and a combine or verification step that accepts wrong output. The best-quantified source (MAST, 2025) attributes about 42% of multi-agent failures to specification, 37% to inter-agent misalignment and 21% to verification, and simple fixes recovered only 9 to 16 points.

### Cited Findings
- **Cross-chunk dependency lost in parallel designs:** in Chain of Agents' ablation, parallel "Merge" and "Hierarchical" variants underperform the sequential chain because workers cannot communicate; chunk order also matters (left-to-right beats reversed or random). — [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)
- **Units inventing answers from irrelevant chunks:** LongAgent reports members fabricate answers when their chunk lacks the information; mitigations were training on unanswerable examples (correct refusals 51.0% to 78.6%) and pairwise chunk exchange between disagreeing members. LLM×MapReduce's equivalent is the explicit "NO INFORMATION" answer that later stages drop. — [arXiv 2402.11550 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11550v2); [arXiv 2410.09342 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342v1)
- **Independence wrongly assumed:** Skeleton-of-Thought ignores dependencies between points and loses quality on math, coding, Fermi and writing questions; its coherence and immersion scores drop while diversity and relevance rise. — [arXiv 2307.15337 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15337v3)
- **Inconsistent entity naming across units:** DocETL has a dedicated Resolve operator and a "duplicate key resolution before reduce" rewrite because units name the same entity differently and grouping then fails. — [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3)
- **Duplicated work:** step repetition is the single most frequent failure mode in MAST at 17.14% of failures. RLM reports one run re-deriving its answer more than five times and then returning a wrong one, and Qwen3-Coder making thousands of sub-calls on basic tasks until a warning was added to the prompt. — [arXiv 2503.13657 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2); [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1)
- **Combine step discards or contradicts unit results:** RLM reports a run that built the correct answer in the REPL and then returned a different answer from the root model. LLMCompiler attributes 28% of its ParallelQA failures to the final output step. Chain of Agents found giving the manager every worker's record was worse than giving it only the last. — [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1); [arXiv 2312.04511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04511v3); [arXiv 2406.02818 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)
- **Weak verification:** MAST's verification category is 21.30% of failures: premature termination 7.82%, no or incomplete verification 6.82%, incorrect verification 6.66%. — [arXiv 2503.13657 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- **Inter-agent misalignment:** 36.94% of MAST failures: reasoning-action mismatch 13.98%, failing to ask for clarification 11.65%, task derailment 7.15%, conversation reset 2.33%, information withholding 1.66%, ignoring another agent's input 0.17%. Specification issues are 41.77%, including disobeying the task specification 10.98% and not knowing when to stop 9.82%. Annotator agreement kappa 0.88; models GPT-4, Claude 3, Qwen2.5, CodeLlama. — [arXiv 2503.13657 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2); [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **Fixes help only partly:** clearer role specifications gave ChatDev +9.4% success (GPT-4o) and an added high-level verification step +15.6% absolute on ProgramDev; the authors say these do not eliminate the failure modes. — [arXiv 2503.13657 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- **Consensus is not routine:** on an open blackboard, full agreement was reached on 89.8% of MMLU problems but only 52.5% of GPQA and 29.4% of MATH, so a combine rule that waits for or assumes agreement fails on hard problems. — [arXiv 2507.01701 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01701v1)
- **Decomposition costs more and can lose to simpler baselines:** the blackboard system costs about 2.3× RAG per question; DocETL's optimised pipelines cost more and run slower; RLM and LCM are at or below the base model on short inputs; on a 7B model a two-call self-refinement beat a five-agent pipeline (86.2% vs 75.0% on GSM8K) at 7.4× fewer tokens. — [arXiv 2510.01285 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01285v2); [arXiv 2410.12189 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12189v3); [arXiv 2512.24601 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v1); [arXiv 2607.26922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26922)
- **Shared-state anomalies:** stale reads and lost updates between agents are argued, not measured, to underlie many coordination failures. — [arXiv 2608.18092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18092)
- **Benchmark contamination inflates decomposition results:** LCM's authors found Opus 4.6 recognised OOLONG data and answered from memory, and the gap narrows in the raw results. — [arXiv 2605.04050 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04050v1)

### Inferences
- "Combine step hallucinating agreement" is not measured directly under that name anywhere I found. The nearest evidence is the low full-consensus rates on hard problems in LbMAS, MAST's incorrect-verification share, and the final-step failures in LLMCompiler and RLM. Together they suggest the join is a real error source, but the specific rate at which a merger papers over a conflict is unquantified.
- The mitigations that have measurements behind them are: an explicit "nothing here" return value; a comparable confidence scale across units; pairing disagreeing units on each other's evidence rather than letting a third party pick; canonicalising entity names before grouping; a router that declines to decompose; a guard that forces each delegation to shrink scope; and doing the reduction in deterministic code.
- MAST's low rates for information withholding (1.66%) and ignored input (0.17%) against high rates for repetition and specification errors suggest that, in 2024 to 2025 frameworks, failures came more from unclear unit contracts and missing stop conditions than from agents refusing to share.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies inconsistent assumptions between parallel units (for example two units choosing incompatible interfaces) as a separate category; MAST's categories do not isolate it.
- MAST's traces are from 2024 to early 2025 models and frameworks; I found no equivalent failure census on 2026 models.
- No measured rate found for a merge step asserting agreement that the unit outputs do not support.
